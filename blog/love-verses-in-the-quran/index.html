Love Verses in the Quran: Mawaddah and Married Love

﷽

In the name of Allah, the Most Merciful, the Bestower of Mercy.

We try to follow and explain from the Qur'an and the authentic Hadith, upon the understanding of the three rightly-guided generations (the Salaf as-Salih). All good is from Allah alone, and any mistake or shortcoming is from ourselves.

TL;DR: The Quran does not speak of love as a stray feeling chased before marriage. It ties love to the nikah itself. The central verse is Qur'an 30:21, where Allah places mawaddah (affection) and rahmah (mercy) between spouses as one of His signs. Alongside it sit the garment verse (Qur'an 2:187), the verse of tranquility (Qur'an 7:189), and the du'a of the righteous (Qur'an 25:74). Love in the Quran is a fruit of a lawful union, not a substitute for it.

Love in the Quran Belongs Inside Marriage

The word people reach for is love, but the Quran is more precise. It uses mawaddah, a tender, willed affection, and pairs it with rahmah, mercy that keeps a marriage soft when the first rush fades. These are not described as things you find in the street or manufacture through months of unsupervised messaging. They are described as a gift Allah settles between a husband and wife after the contract binds them.

This matters because a great deal of modern advice inverts the order. It tells people to secure the feeling first and formalise it later. The Quran does the opposite. It presents love as one of the outcomes of a rightly built marriage, which is why the search for a spouse should be a search for character and deen, not a search for a spark. If you are at that stage, our guide on how to get married in Islam walks through the lawful path from start to finish.

Qur'an 30:21: The Verse of Mawaddah and Rahmah

The anchor for this entire subject is the statement of Allah:

"And among His signs is that He created for you from yourselves mates that you may find tranquillity in them, and He placed between you affection and mercy. Indeed in that are signs for a people who reflect." (Qur'an 30:21)

Three things are worth pausing on. First, the purpose given is sakinah, tranquillity, a settled calm rather than restless excitement. Second, the affection and mercy are said to be placed by Allah, meaning the deepest bond in a marriage is His doing, not merely the couple's chemistry. Third, this is called a sign, put in the same category as the creation of the heavens and the earth. A stable, loving marriage is meant to point the spouses back to their Lord.

The Prophet ﷺ made the same connection between marriage and this kind of love when he said, as reported in an authentic narration, "We have not seen anything better for two who love one another than marriage" (Sunan Ibn Majah 1847). Marriage is the vessel Allah designed to hold love, not the paperwork you tolerate after the love already happened.

The Garment Verse: Qur'an 2:187

Allah describes spouses with one of the most intimate images in the Quran:

"They are a garment for you and you are a garment for them." (Qur'an 2:187)

A garment is close to the skin. It covers what should not be exposed, it protects from harm, it beautifies, and it is worn for comfort. Each spouse is told to be this for the other. The verse quietly destroys the idea that marriage is a transaction between two independent parties. It is mutual covering. A husband conceals his wife's faults and shields her from hardship, and she does the same for him. This is love expressed as protection and discretion, not as public display.

Tranquillity and the Single Soul: Qur'an 7:189

Allah traces marital affection back to the very design of creation:

"It is He who created you from one soul and made from it its mate that he might find tranquillity in her." (Qur'an 7:189)

The longing a spouse feels is written into human origin. Man and woman were made to complete a kind of rest in each other. Notice again the word tranquillity. The Quran keeps returning to peace, not intensity. A marriage measured only by how strongly two people feel will always be fragile. A marriage aimed at sakinah can carry affection through illness, poverty, children, and old age.

The Du'a of the Righteous: Qur'an 25:74

When the Quran describes the servants of the Most Merciful, it records the du'a they make about their families:

"And those who say, 'Our Lord, grant us from among our spouses and offspring comfort to our eyes, and make us leaders for the righteous.'" (Qur'an 25:74)

The phrase "comfort to our eyes" (qurrata a'yun) is an idiom for something that brings deep, settling joy. Look at what the righteous ask for. Not wealth from their spouses, not status, but that their spouse and children be a source of coolness for the eyes and a means to lead in righteousness. This is love with a direction. It wants the beloved for the sake of the Hereafter, not only for this world. Choosing that kind of spouse begins with the criteria in our guide on choosing a spouse in Islam.

Love Ordered Rightly: Allah and His Messenger First

The Quran never lets love of a person outrank love of Allah. This is not cold. It is the safeguard that keeps married love from becoming an idol.

"But those who believe are stronger in love for Allah." (Qur'an 2:165)

And the proof of loving Allah is following His Messenger ﷺ:

"Say, 'If you love Allah, then follow me, and Allah will love you and forgive you your sins.'" (Qur'an 3:31)

A marriage founded on obedience to Allah is a marriage where the two people love each other for Allah, and that love is protected by the strongest bond there is. The Prophet ﷺ taught that among those Allah shades on the Day of Judgement are "two people who loved each other for Allah's sake, meeting for that and parting upon that" (Sahih al-Bukhari 660). When love is anchored above the beloved, it survives the beloved's flaws.

How the Sunnah Shows Married Love

The verses describe love; the Sunnah shows it in action. The Prophet ﷺ was openly affectionate and gentle with his wives, and he set the standard plainly:

"The best of you is the best to his family, and I am the best of you to my family." (Jami' at-Tirmidhi 3895)

Affection is not a private extra on top of the deen. It is part of a man's righteousness. In another authentic report, when 'Aisha رضي الله عنها asked the Prophet ﷺ how his love for her was, he compared it to a firm, unbroken knot, and this closeness between them is recorded across the two Sahihs. Married love in the Sunnah is warm, spoken, and lived, expressed through kindness, patience, service, and time.

And the Quran commands that the affection be practical, especially from the husband:

"And live with them in kindness." (Qur'an 4:19)

Good treatment is obligatory even on the days the feeling runs thin. That is precisely why Allah pairs mawaddah with rahmah. Mercy carries a couple across the seasons when affection alone would not.

Building Mawaddah the Halal Way

So where does love come from if not from dating? From a lawful process that Allah blesses. The order is: sincere intention, a search based on deen and character, involvement of the woman's wali, a serious and supervised getting-to-know within the limits of the Shariah, and then the nikah. The love described in Qur'an 30:21 is placed by Allah after that door is opened, not before it.

The Prophet ﷺ told the man proposing to look, in the sense the Shariah permits, and pointed to the decisive criterion in his famous instruction to marry for deen: "marry the religious one, may your hands be rubbed with dust" (Sahih al-Bukhari 5090). Deen is what predicts rahmah. A God-fearing spouse forgives, keeps secrets, and stays when things are hard, and that is the raw material of lasting affection. Do not confuse the fireworks of an unlawful relationship with the mawaddah of the Quran. One burns out and leaves regret. The other is a sign of Allah, placed in a home He blesses, growing quietly over decades. Aim for the second.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the main love verses in the Quran about marriage?

The clearest are Qur'an 30:21 on mawaddah and rahmah, Qur'an 2:187 describing spouses as garments for each other, Qur'an 7:189 on finding tranquillity in one's mate, Qur'an 25:74 on spouses being comfort to the eyes, and Qur'an 4:19 commanding kind treatment.

What does mawaddah mean?

Mawaddah is a warm, willed affection between spouses. In Qur'an 30:21 Allah says He places it between husband and wife, alongside rahmah (mercy). Affection carries the good days; mercy carries the difficult ones. Together they hold a marriage across a lifetime.

Does the Quran encourage romantic love?

The Quran affirms deep affection and tranquillity between spouses and calls it a sign of Allah. What it does not endorse is pursuing romance outside a lawful marriage through dating, free-mixing, or private relationships. The love it praises is love inside the nikah, expressed with kindness as commanded in Qur'an 4:19.

How do I find love in an Islamic marriage before I even marry?

You do not chase the feeling first. You seek a spouse of deen and good character, involve the wali, and keep the process within the Shariah. The Prophet ﷺ said to prioritise religion when choosing (Sahih al-Bukhari 5090). The love described in Qur'an 30:21 is placed by Allah once the marriage begins.

Is it wrong to love my spouse more than I love worship?

Love of Allah must come first, as in Qur'an 2:165. But loving your spouse is not in competition with that when the marriage is built on obedience to Allah. The Prophet ﷺ praised those who love one another for Allah's sake (Sahih al-Bukhari 660). Order your love rightly and it will only grow.

Which hadith best captures married love?

The Prophet ﷺ said, "The best of you is the best to his family, and I am the best of you to my family" (Jami' at-Tirmidhi 3895). It makes affection and good treatment a measure of a person's faith, not an optional extra.
