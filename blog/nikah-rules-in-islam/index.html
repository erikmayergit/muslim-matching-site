Nikah Rules in Islam: The Conditions of a Valid Marriage

﷽

In the name of Allah, the Most Merciful, the Bestower of Mercy.

We try to follow and explain from the Qur'an and the authentic Hadith, upon the understanding of the three rightly-guided generations (the Salaf as-Salih). All good is from Allah alone, and any mistake or shortcoming is from ourselves.

TL;DR: A nikah is valid in Islam when five things are present: the free consent of both spouses, a walī (guardian) contracting on the woman's behalf, two upright Muslim witnesses, an agreed mahr, and a clear offer and acceptance between the two parties. Miss the walī or the witnesses and the contract is defective, not a marriage.

Marriage in Islam is not a private arrangement between two people who feel ready. It is a contract with clear conditions, and Allah tied great tranquility and mercy to it when it is done His way. He said, "And among His signs is that He created for you spouses from among yourselves that you may find tranquility in them, and He placed between you affection and mercy" (Qur'an 30:21). That tranquility comes from doing the thing correctly, on the terms Allah and His Messenger laid down, not from bypassing them because they feel inconvenient.

Below are the actual nikah rules in Islam: the pillars that must be present, the conditions that make each one count, and the mistakes that quietly invalidate a marriage people assumed was sound.

What Makes a Nikah Valid

A valid nikah rests on pillars (arkān) and conditions (shurūṭ). If a pillar is missing, there is no contract at all. If a condition is broken, the contract is defective and may need to be corrected or redone.

The core elements the scholars derive from the Qur'an and Sunnah are:

  1. Two parties who are lawful for each other to marry.
  2. The genuine consent of both the man and the woman.
  3. A walī (guardian) who contracts the marriage for the woman.
  4. Two upright Muslim witnesses.
  5. A specified mahr (dower) for the wife.
  6. A clear ījāb and qabūl, the offer and its acceptance, in one sitting.

Each of these deserves its own explanation, because most disputes about whether a marriage "counts" come down to one of them being skipped. For the bigger picture of the whole process, see our guide on how to get married in Islam.

The Consent of Both Spouses

No one is married into a nikah against their will. The woman's consent is a condition, not a formality. The Prophet (peace be upon him) said, "A previously married woman is not to be married until she is consulted, and a virgin is not to be married until her permission is sought" (Sahih al-Bukhari 5136). The Companions asked how her permission is known, and he said her silence is her permission, because of her shyness.

So a father, however well-meaning, cannot force a daughter into a marriage she refuses. A woman came to the Prophet (peace be upon him) reporting that her father had married her off without her consent, and he gave her the choice to keep the marriage or annul it (reported in Sunan Ibn Majah 1875). Consent must be real. The man's consent is equally required; no one is validly married while coerced.

This is the balance the Shariah strikes. The woman is protected by a guardian who negotiates and safeguards her interests, and she is protected from that guardian abusing his role, because her refusal stands.

The Walī: The Woman's Guardian

The walī is the condition most often attacked or quietly dropped today, so be clear about it: a woman does not contract her own nikah. Her guardian does it for her, with her consent. The Prophet (peace be upon him) said, "There is no marriage without a walī" (Jami' at-Tirmidhi 1101, authentic). He also said, "Any woman who marries without the permission of her walī, her marriage is invalid, her marriage is invalid, her marriage is invalid" (Sunan Abu Dawud 2083, authentic), repeating it three times for emphasis.

The walī is normally the father, then the paternal grandfather, then a son, then brothers, then paternal uncles, in a known order of nearness. His job is not to overrule her but to represent her and protect her from a poor match. He should not refuse a suitable, religious suitor without valid reason; if a guardian is unjustly obstructive (ʿaḍl), that guardianship can pass to the next eligible guardian or to the Muslim authority. A woman with no Muslim relatives takes the local imam or a trustworthy authority as her walī.

We explain the guardian's role, order, and limits in more detail in our guide on the role of the wali in marriage. Do not treat this condition as optional cultural baggage. It is textually established, and a marriage arranged without it is not a marriage.

Two Upright Witnesses

A nikah is announced and witnessed, never secret. The contract requires two upright, sane, adult Muslim witnesses to the offer and acceptance. The meaning conveyed in the narrations is that there is no valid marriage except with a walī and two trustworthy witnesses, which is why the Companions and the scholars after them treated secret marriage as impermissible.

Umar ibn al-Khattab, when a marriage was reported to him that only one man and one woman had witnessed, said this was a secret marriage and he did not permit it. Witnessing distinguishes a lawful nikah from a private, hidden relationship, which the Shariah shuts the door on completely. This is one reason a purely online "marriage" with no walī and no witnesses is not a nikah, no matter what the two people call it.

The Mahr: The Wife's Right

The mahr is a gift of wealth the husband gives the wife, and it is her property alone, not her father's and not the family's. Allah commanded it directly: "And give the women their dowries graciously" (Qur'an 4:4). It is a condition of the marriage, though the exact amount is open.

The Sunnah is to keep it easy, not to compete over it. The Prophet (peace be upon him) said, "The best of marriages is the one that is easiest" (Sunan Abu Dawud 2117, authentic). He married a man to a woman for a mahr of teaching her the Qur'an he knew, when the man had nothing else to give (Sahih al-Bukhari 5087). It can be gold, money, property, or a benefit of real value, paid immediately or deferred by agreement. What it cannot be is nothing, and it cannot be seized from her against her will. Families that inflate the mahr into a barrier to marriage are working against the Sunnah, not with it. We go deeper in our guide on understanding the mahr.

The Offer and Acceptance

The contract itself is the ījāb and qabūl: the walī offers the woman in marriage ("I marry her to you") and the man accepts ("I accept"). This must be clear, unconditional in a way that contradicts the contract, and it should take place in the same sitting so that offer and acceptance are joined. Both parties, or their agents, must intend a permanent marriage.

This is why time-limited "marriages" are not valid nikah. A marriage contracted with a fixed end date (mutʿah) was forbidden by the Prophet (peace be upon him), as reported in Sahih Muslim 1406. A valid nikah is entered as a permanent bond, even if Allah later decrees it ends in divorce.

Who You Are Not Allowed to Marry

A nikah is only valid between two people lawful to each other. Allah listed the permanently forbidden categories: mothers, daughters, sisters, paternal and maternal aunts, nieces, mothers-in-law, and others, in Qur'an 4:23. Foster (breastfeeding) relations create the same barriers as blood relations. A man cannot be married to two sisters at once, nor to a woman and her aunt at the same time (Sahih al-Bukhari 5109).

A Muslim woman marries only a Muslim man. A Muslim man may marry a chaste Muslim woman or a chaste woman of the People of the Book, though marrying a practising Muslim woman of good deen is far better and safer for the household and the children. Deen is the criterion the Prophet (peace be upon him) told us to prioritise: "A woman is married for four things: her wealth, her lineage, her beauty, and her religion. So choose the one of religion, may your hands be dusted with success" (Sahih al-Bukhari 5090). That instruction to weigh character and deen above everything else is exactly why we built Muslim Matching around intention rather than appearance.

Announcing the Marriage and the Walima

While the walima (wedding feast) is not a condition of validity, publicising the nikah is part of the Sunnah and the opposite of a hidden relationship. The Prophet (peace be upon him) told Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf, when he learned he had married, "Give a walima, even with one sheep" (Sahih al-Bukhari 5167). Making the marriage known protects both spouses and their future children and removes any suspicion.

Keep the celebration within the bounds of the Shariah: no free-mixing that leads to fitnah, no extravagance, no forbidden entertainment, and no cultural customs presented as if they were part of the religion. The marriage is a worship and a means to chastity, so its beginning should not open with disobedience.

Common Mistakes That Invalidate or Weaken a Nikah

  • Contracting the marriage with no walī for the woman, or with a walī who has no right to that role while a nearer one is available and unjustly bypassed.
  • No witnesses, or unislamic "witnesses" who do not meet the conditions, turning it into a secret union.
  • Coercing either party, especially the bride, so that real consent is absent.
  • Setting a fixed end date, which makes it the forbidden mutʿah, not a nikah.
  • Denying the wife her mahr or treating it as the family's money.
  • "Talking stages" and unsupervised courtship dressed up as getting to know each other before any contract exists. There is no lawful private relationship before the nikah; the contract is what makes the two lawful to one another.

Get the pillars right and the rest is detail. Get them wrong and you may be living in a relationship that Allah does not recognise as a marriage, which is a serious matter to correct quickly.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a nikah valid without a walī?

No. For a woman, the walī is a condition. The Prophet (peace be upon him) said there is no marriage without a walī and declared a marriage without the guardian's permission invalid, repeating it three times (Sunan Abu Dawud 2083). A woman does not contract her own nikah.

How many witnesses does a nikah need?

Two upright, sane, adult Muslim witnesses to the offer and acceptance. Witnessing separates a lawful, announced marriage from a forbidden secret relationship, which is why the Companions rejected marriages that were kept hidden.

Is there a minimum amount for the mahr?

There is no fixed maximum, and the Sunnah is to keep it easy. It must be something of real value that the wife owns. The Prophet (peace be upon him) accepted very modest dowers, even teaching Qur'an, and praised the marriage that is easiest to arrange (Sunan Abu Dawud 2117).

Can a father force his daughter to marry?

No. Her consent is required, and a woman whose father married her off without her agreement was given the right by the Prophet (peace be upon him) to annul it (Sunan Ibn Majah 1875). A guardian represents and protects her; he does not overrule her refusal.

Is an online nikah valid?

The validity depends on the pillars being met in reality: a real walī consenting, two witnesses present to the contract, a specified mahr, and clear offer and acceptance in one sitting. If any of these is missing, calling it a nikah does not make it one. Where the setup is unusual, ask a qualified scholar rather than assume.

Does the marriage need to be registered with the government?

Civil registration is not a Shariah condition of validity; the nikah is valid when its pillars and conditions are met. That said, registering the marriage protects the rights of both spouses and any children under the law of the land, so it is strongly advisable where possible.
