The Nikah Ceremony, Step by Step

﷽

In the name of Allah, the Most Merciful, the Bestower of Mercy.

We try to follow and explain from the Qur'an and the authentic Hadith, upon the understanding of the three rightly-guided generations (the Salaf as-Salih). All good is from Allah alone, and any mistake or shortcoming is from ourselves.

TL;DR: A valid nikah ceremony rests on a few essentials, not a long ritual: the woman's walī, two trustworthy Muslim witnesses, a clear offer and acceptance, the woman's consent, and an agreed mahr. Everything else (the khutbah, the gathering, the walīmah) is either recommended or simply custom. Get the essentials right and the marriage is valid in the sight of Allah.

Many people arrive at their wedding worried about the wrong things. They fret over the hall, the guest list, and the food, and they have only a vague idea of what actually makes their nikah valid. This guide fixes that. It walks through the nikah ceremony the way the Shariah defines it, separates the binding essentials from the recommended sunnah and from mere culture, and gives you a clear order of events.

What the Nikah Actually Is

Nikah is a solemn contract (aqd) between a man and a woman that makes marital life lawful and builds a household on the pleasure of Allah. It is not a formality to be rushed through, nor a cultural festival with a signature at the end. Allah calls it a mithaqan ghaliza, a solemn covenant (Qur'an 4:21).

The purpose is stated plainly: "And among His signs is that He created for you spouses from among yourselves that you may find tranquillity in them, and He placed between you affection and mercy" (Qur'an 30:21). Allah also commands the community to help singles marry: "And marry the unmarried among you and the righteous among your male and female slaves" (Qur'an 24:32).

The Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) tied marriage directly to preserving one's religion. In a sound narration he said, "O young men, whoever among you is able to marry, let him marry, for it lowers the gaze and guards the private parts" (Sahih al-Bukhari 5065). So the ceremony is not the point. The lawful, God-conscious union it creates is the point.

The Essentials That Make a Nikah Valid

If any of these is missing, you do not have a valid marriage, no matter how large the gathering. If you are still at the earlier stage of choosing a spouse and want that process to be clean and serious, our guide on how to get married in Islam covers it.

1. The walī (the woman's guardian)

A woman is not married off by herself. Her marriage is contracted by her walī, normally her father, then her grandfather, then her nearest suitable male relative in order. This is not an optional custom; it is a condition of validity. The Prophet said, "There is no marriage without a walī" (Sunan Abi Dawud 2085, authentic). He also said, "Any woman who marries without the permission of her walī, her marriage is invalid, invalid, invalid" (Sunan Abi Dawud 2083; also Jami' at-Tirmidhi 1102, authentic).

Do not let anyone talk you out of this. Weakening the walī requirement is one of the clearest signs a source has drifted from the Sunnah into modernist reinterpretation. If a woman has no father or male relatives, the authority (a Muslim judge or, in his absence, a trusted person of authority in the community) acts as her walī, because the Prophet said, "The ruler is the walī of the one who has no walī" (Jami' at-Tirmidhi 1102).

2. Two trustworthy Muslim witnesses

The contract must be witnessed. The Prophet said, "There is no marriage except with a walī and two trustworthy witnesses" (reported with authentic chains; the meaning is well established across the narrations of the walī hadith). Witnesses distinguish a lawful nikah from a secret affair and protect the rights of both spouses and any future children.

3. The offer and acceptance (ijab and qabul)

The contract is concluded by a clear offer from the walī's side and a clear acceptance from the groom, spoken in the same sitting and heard by the witnesses. For example the walī says, "I marry her to you," and the groom answers, "I accept." There is no fixed magic wording required in Arabic or otherwise. What matters is that the offer and acceptance are unambiguous, immediate, and understood by those present.

4. The woman's genuine consent

A woman cannot be forced. The Prophet said, "A previously married woman should not be married until she is consulted, and a virgin should not be married until her permission is sought." They asked how her permission is known, and he said, "By her silence" (Sahih al-Bukhari 5136). A woman who was married against her will came to the Prophet and he gave her the choice to annul it (Sunan Abi Dawud 2096). Consent is real, and it is required.

5. The mahr

The mahr is the bridal gift the groom gives the wife. It is her right and her property alone, not her father's and not a family fund. "And give the women their bridal gift graciously" (Qur'an 4:4). We cover this in detail below.

The Nikah Ceremony, Step by Step

Here is a straightforward order for the gathering. Steps one and five are recommended sunnah; the middle steps are the binding core.

Step 1: The khutbah (khutbat al-hajah)

It is from the Sunnah to open the gathering with a short sermon of need, the khutbat al-hajah that the Prophet taught his companions. It begins, "Indeed all praise is due to Allah. We praise Him, seek His help and His forgiveness..." and includes three verses of taqwa (Qur'an 3:102, Qur'an 4:1, and Qur'an 33:70). This is reported in Sunan Abi Dawud 2118 and graded authentic. It is a beautiful sunnah, but note: it is recommended, not a condition. The nikah is valid without it.

Step 2: The agreement on the mahr

Before or at the sitting, the mahr is named and agreed. It should be stated openly so the witnesses hear it and the wife knows exactly what is hers.

Step 3: The offer and acceptance before witnesses

With the walī, the groom, and the two witnesses present, the walī offers his ward in marriage and the groom accepts. This is the moment the contract is concluded. Once the ijab and qabul are complete and heard, they are married.

Step 4: Recording and signing

Writing the contract down is not a religious pillar, but it is wise and often legally necessary. A written record protects the mahr, the conditions either party stipulated, and the rights of both spouses. If you want to understand what to put in it and which conditions are valid, see our guide to the nikah contract.

Step 5: Du'a and congratulation

After the contract, make du'a for the couple. The Prophet used to congratulate a newly married man with the words, "May Allah bless you, and send blessings upon you, and join you both in good" (Sunan Abi Dawud 2130, authentic). Simple, sincere, and free of the elaborate customs many cultures pile on.

The Mahr Done Right

The mahr belongs to the wife. It can be money, gold, property, or even teaching, as long as it is something of real value that she accepts. In one famous case the Prophet married a man to a woman on the condition that he teach her the chapters of the Qur'an he knew, because he genuinely had nothing else to give (Sahih al-Bukhari 5087).

The lesson is not that mahr should be trivial, but that it should not be a barrier. Do not let families turn the mahr into a bidding war that delays or prevents marriage. Ease is praised. The Prophet said, "The best of mahr is the easiest" (reported in the collections; the principle of ease in marriage is firmly established in the Sunnah). Name a mahr the wife is content with, that the husband can actually pay, and settle it clearly.

The Walīmah

The walīmah is the marriage feast the husband hosts to announce and celebrate the union. It is a strongly encouraged sunnah. When the Prophet saw signs of marriage on Abd ar-Rahman ibn Awf, he told him, "Hold a walīmah, even with one sheep" (Sahih al-Bukhari 5167).

Keep it within your means. A walīmah is meant to feed people and make the marriage public, not to bury a young couple in debt. Accepting an invitation to a walīmah is itself part of the Sunnah. Announcing the marriage openly is also encouraged, so that the union is known and distinguished from anything secret.

What to Leave Out: Culture Is Not the Deen

A great deal of what people think of as a "proper" wedding is culture, and some of it is outright forbidden. Keep the ceremony clean of:

  • Free-mixing and immodesty. A gathering that collapses the boundaries Allah set is a poor foundation for a marriage built on His pleasure. Keep the arrangements modest and within the Shariah rather than following whatever the culture normalises.
  • Music, instruments, and displays that contradict the Sunnah. The lawful, encouraged form of announcing a wedding is simple and modest.
  • Extravagance and debt. Allah does not love the wasteful (Qur'an 7:31).
  • Cultural practices dressed up as religion, such as obligatory dowry demands on the bride's family, or treating engagement customs as if they were binding contracts.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the nikah valid without a big ceremony?

Yes. The ceremony, the hall, and the feast are not what make a nikah valid. The essentials are the walī, two trustworthy witnesses, a clear offer and acceptance, the woman's consent, and an agreed mahr. A simple nikah with these is fully valid, and the Prophet praised ease in marriage.

Can a woman marry herself off without a walī?

No. The Prophet said, "There is no marriage without a walī" (Sunan Abi Dawud 2085), and he declared a marriage contracted without the walī's permission invalid (Jami' at-Tirmidhi 1102). If a woman has no relatives to act as walī, the Muslim authority does. Any source telling you to skip the walī has left the guidance of the Sunnah.

Who can be a witness at a nikah?

Two trustworthy, sane, adult Muslim men who can hear and understand the offer and acceptance. Their role is to make the marriage a known, public contract rather than a secret arrangement.

Does the mahr have to be expensive?

No. The mahr is the wife's right, but the Sunnah is ease. It should be something of value she accepts and the husband can genuinely give. It should never become a barrier that delays or prevents a righteous marriage.

Is the khutbat al-hajah required for the nikah to be valid?

No. Opening with the khutbat al-hajah is a recommended sunnah reported authentically (Sunan Abi Dawud 2118), and it is beautiful to include, but the contract is valid without it once the essentials are met.

What is the difference between the nikah and the walīmah?

The nikah is the marriage contract itself, the binding aqd with its essentials. The walīmah is the feast the husband holds afterward to announce and celebrate the marriage, a strongly encouraged sunnah (Sahih al-Bukhari 5167). One creates the marriage; the other publicises and honours it.
