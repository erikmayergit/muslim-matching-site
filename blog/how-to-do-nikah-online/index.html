How To Do Nikah Online: A Deen-First Guide

﷽

In the name of Allah, the Most Merciful, the Bestower of Mercy.

We try to follow and explain from the Qur'an and the authentic Hadith, upon the understanding of the three rightly-guided generations (the Salaf as-Salih). All good is from Allah alone, and any mistake or shortcoming is from ourselves.

TL;DR: An online nikah can be valid when the same conditions of a normal nikah are met: the woman's walī, two upright Muslim witnesses, a clear offer and acceptance (ijāb and qabūl) in one connected session, an agreed mahr, and genuine consent. The video link is only the means of connecting people. It does not remove a single Shar'ī condition, and where scholars differ on remote sessions you should follow a qualified scholar rather than assume.

Marriage over a video call became common out of necessity: families separated by borders, a spouse abroad, visa delays, or a couple simply wanting to close the door on anything haram quickly. The question is not whether it is convenient. The question is whether the nikah is actually valid in the sight of Allah. That depends entirely on getting the pillars right, and those pillars are the same online as they are in a masjid.

What an online nikah actually is

An online nikah is not a different kind of marriage. It is an ordinary Islamic marriage contract in which some or all of the parties are connected by live video or audio rather than sitting in one physical room. The walī may be in one country, the groom in another, and the witnesses wherever they are needed. Nothing about the technology changes the deen. A nikah is a serious covenant, described in the Qur'an as a mīthāqan ghalīẓā, a solemn pledge (Qur'an 4:21). Allah also reminds the believers of the mercy He placed in marriage: "And among His signs is that He created for you spouses from among yourselves, that you may find tranquillity in them, and He placed between you affection and mercy" (Qur'an 30:21).

So when people ask how to do nikah online, the honest answer is: you do a proper nikah, and you use the screen only to bridge distance. If any pillar is missing, the marriage is invalid whether it happened online or in person.

The pillars that do not change online

These conditions are not optional extras. Drop one and there is no valid nikah at all. Get them right and the contract stands whether the people are in one room or on five screens.

The walī (guardian)

A woman does not marry herself off, and she does not appoint a random man to do it. Her nikah is contracted by her walī: her father, then grandfather, then closest male relatives in order. The Prophet (ﷺ) said, "There is no marriage except with a walī" (Sunan Abi Dawud 2085, authentic). He also said, "Any woman who marries without the permission of her walī, her marriage is invalid, invalid, invalid" (Sunan Abi Dawud 2083, authentic).

This is a firm condition and no amount of distance removes it. If the walī cannot be physically present, he can be present on the call and give his consent live. If a woman genuinely has no walī, or her walī unjustly refuses a suitable proposal, the authority passes according to the well-known order and, in cases of unjust refusal, to the Muslim judge or authority. Do not let anyone online tell you the walī can simply be skipped. That is exactly the kind of shortcut that produces a marriage that is not a marriage. You can read more in our guide to the nikah contract.

Two upright witnesses

A valid nikah is announced and witnessed, not hidden. The scholars are agreed that the marriage must be witnessed, and the established position upon the evidence is that there must be at least two trustworthy Muslim men who hear the offer and acceptance. This is what separates a lawful marriage from a secret arrangement. In an online nikah the witnesses must actually be able to hear the walī and the groom clearly and be able to identify who is contracting. If the connection drops or the witnesses cannot confirm who is speaking, the witnessing is defective.

Ijāb and qabūl in one connected session

The core act of the contract is the offer (ijāb) from the walī and the acceptance (qabūl) from the groom, using clear words of marriage. "I marry you my daughter so-and-so for the agreed mahr," and the reply, "I accept this marriage." These two statements must happen in one connected sitting (majlis) with no long, unrelated interruption between them.

Online, the majlis is created by the live connection itself. As long as everyone is joined to the same live call and the offer and acceptance follow one another directly and are heard by the witnesses, the requirement of one sitting is met. This is where a live video or audio call is stronger than exchanging text messages or recordings, because everyone is present and responding in real time.

The mahr

The mahr is the woman's right, given by the groom to her, not to her family. Allah commands, "And give the women their mahr as a free gift" (Qur'an 4:4). It should be agreed and named before or during the contract. It can be modest; the Prophet (ﷺ) said, "The best of marriage is the easiest of it" in meaning, and he married people for very small dowries. What matters is that it is real, agreed, and understood by both sides and the witnesses. See our guide on choosing a righteous spouse for how to keep expectations balanced.

Genuine consent

Neither party may be forced. The Prophet (ﷺ) said, "A previously married woman shall not be married until she is consulted, and a virgin shall not be married until her permission is sought" (Sahih al-Bukhari 5136). Consent given under pressure, or without the person even knowing the terms, is not real consent. Online this matters even more, because it is easier for a party to be coached off-screen. Everyone on the call should be satisfied that the bride and groom are freely agreeing.

Is an online nikah valid? Where scholars differ

The conditions above are agreed. The disputed part is narrower: can the walī, groom, and witnesses be in physically separate locations and still count as one majlis, and is video reliable enough to establish who is contracting?

Many contemporary scholars and fatwa bodies have permitted marriage by live video when identity is confirmed and the offer and acceptance are heard clearly by the witnesses, treating the live call as a valid single sitting. Others are more cautious and prefer that the witnesses be physically present with the parties, or that at least the walī and witnesses sit together while only the groom is remote. This is a real difference of opinion among people of knowledge, and it is not something to settle from an article.

The safe, honest position is this: do not treat the disputed detail as if it were closed. If you want maximum certainty, arrange for the witnesses to be physically present with the walī, and let only the groom (or only the bride's side) join by video. That satisfies both camps. If full remote contracting is your only option, put the exact arrangement to a qualified scholar who follows the Qur'an and Sunnah upon the understanding of the Salaf, and act on his ruling. Do not let convenience decide a matter of validity.

How to do nikah online, step by step

  1. Confirm the walī. Identify the correct guardian in order and make sure he agrees to conduct the marriage. If there is a dispute over the walī, resolve it before anything else.
  2. Agree the mahr. Settle the amount and form of the mahr in advance so it can be stated clearly during the contract.
  3. Arrange two upright witnesses. Choose trustworthy Muslim men who know, or can verify, who the walī and groom are.
  4. Decide the setup. For the strongest position, keep the walī and witnesses in one room and connect the groom by video. Confirm the platform, the time, and a backup line in case the connection fails.
  5. Verify identities live. At the start of the call, everyone should confirm who each person is, out loud, so the witnesses can testify with certainty.
  6. Perform the ijāb and qabūl. The walī makes the offer with clear words of marriage and the named mahr. The groom accepts immediately, in the same connected session, in words the witnesses hear.
  7. Document it. Write and, where possible, register the contract. Islamic registration and civil registration protect the woman's rights, especially across borders.
  8. Announce it. Marriage is meant to be public, not secret. Let it be known among family and community.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Skipping or sidelining the walī. No walī, no nikah. A woman marrying herself off over a call is not married.
  • Weak or missing witnessing. Two friends half-listening on a laggy line is not sound witnessing. They must clearly hear and identify the parties.
  • Treating chat messages as a contract. Ijāb and qabūl need a live, connected session, not a delayed exchange of texts or voice notes.
  • No identity verification. Without confirming who is on screen, the whole contract is exposed to doubt or fraud.
  • Secret marriages. Hiding the nikah from family goes against its public nature and usually signals a problem you should not paper over.
  • Assuming the disputed points are settled. If you are contracting fully remotely, get a scholar's ruling first.

When online nikah is not appropriate

An online nikah is a tool for distance, not a tool for avoiding accountability. If it is being used to keep a marriage hidden from a father who would rightly object, or to rush two people together after unsupervised private contact, the tool is being misused. The goal of marrying is to obey Allah and build a home upon the deen, as the Prophet (ﷺ) taught when he said to prioritise the one with religion (Sahih al-Bukhari 5090). Anything that starts by cutting corners on the Shariah is not a strong foundation, screen or no screen.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a nikah done over video call valid? It can be, when the walī, two upright witnesses, clear offer and acceptance in one connected session, mahr, and consent are all present and the identities are confirmed. Scholars differ on fully remote setups, so for certainty keep the witnesses present with the walī and consult a qualified scholar.

Can the bride marry herself online without a walī? No. The walī is a condition of the nikah, in person or online. The Prophet (ﷺ) said there is no marriage without a walī (Sunan Abi Dawud 2085). Distance does not remove this.

Do the witnesses need to be in the same room as the couple? They must clearly hear and identify the parties and the contract. The most cautious position, agreed on by all sides of the dispute, is to have the witnesses physically present with the walī while only the other party joins remotely.

Is a text or WhatsApp nikah valid? A delayed exchange of messages does not establish the one connected sitting the contract needs, and identity is far harder to confirm. A live call is far stronger. Do not rely on written messages as the contract itself.

Does an online nikah need to be registered? Registration is not a pillar, but documenting and registering the marriage, both Islamically and civilly where possible, protects the woman's rights and the children's, especially across borders. It is strongly advised. See our nikah contract guide.

What if my walī refuses a suitable, righteous proposal? A walī may not unjustly block a compatible, God-fearing suitor. If he does, the matter passes according to the recognised order of guardianship, and in cases of unjust refusal to the Muslim authority or judge. Bring this to a qualified scholar rather than acting alone.
