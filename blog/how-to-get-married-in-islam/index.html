How to Get Married in Islam: Step by Step

﷽

In the name of Allah, the Most Merciful, the Bestower of Mercy.

We try to follow and explain from the Qur'an and the authentic Hadith, upon the understanding of the three rightly-guided generations (the Salaf as-Salih). All good is from Allah alone, and any mistake or shortcoming is from ourselves.

TL;DR: Marrying in Islam is a clear, dignified process: form a sincere intention, make du'a and istikhara, search through halal channels with a wali involved from the start, meet for a serious purpose under Shariah boundaries, agree the mahr, sign the nikah contract before witnesses, and announce it with a walima. No dating, no free-mixing, no shortcuts.

If you want to know how to get married in Islam without the confusion and the cultural baggage, this guide walks the whole road from the first intention to the walima. The process is simpler than people make it. Most of the difficulty comes from mixing in customs that are not from the deen, or skipping the obligations that are.

Step 1: Correct Your Intention

Everything starts with why you are marrying. The Prophet (peace be upon him) said that actions are judged by intentions, as reported in Sahih al-Bukhari 1. Marriage in Islam is an act of worship, not just a social arrangement. You are seeking to complete half of your religion, to guard your chastity, to build a home where Allah is obeyed, and to raise children upon the deen.

Allah says that He created spouses so you may find tranquillity in them, and placed love and mercy between them, in Qur'an 30:21. That is the goal. When the intention is right, the priorities fall into place: you look for deen and character first, not status or wealth or family prestige.

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said a woman is married for four things, and instructed: choose the one of religion, as in Sahih al-Bukhari 5090. The same standard applies to choosing a husband. When someone of pleasing religion and character proposes, the guidance is to accept, as narrated in Sunan al-Tirmidhi 1084.

Step 2: Make Du'a and Pray Istikhara

Marriage is one of the biggest decisions you will make, so take it back to Allah. Ask Him in du'a to grant you a righteous spouse and to make the path easy. When a concrete option appears and you have done your due diligence, pray salat al-istikhara, the prayer for seeking Allah's guidance, taught by the Prophet (peace be upon him) in Sahih al-Bukhari 1166.

Istikhara is not a request for a dream or a feeling. It is asking Allah to make easy what is good for you and to turn you away from what is harmful. After praying it, you continue to act, gather information, and move forward. Allah arranges the outcome.

Step 3: Search Through Halal Channels

This is where most people go wrong today. The Islamic way of finding a spouse is not casual conversation, a "talking stage", or unsupervised meetings. It is a purposeful, transparent search where families are involved early and the goal of marriage is stated openly from the start.

Legitimate channels include trusted family and friends who know your character and the other person's, the imam or knowledgeable people in your community, and serious matrimonial platforms built for marriage rather than entertainment. A platform that keeps things intention-first and brings the wali into the process keeps you within the boundaries. If you are looking for that kind of structure, you can create your Muslim Matching profile and search with seriousness rather than drift.

What to avoid is equally clear. Private, ongoing, unsupervised messaging where two people grow attached before any commitment is the door to fitnah. The Prophet (peace be upon him) warned that a man is never alone with a woman except that Shaytan is the third of them, as narrated in Sunan al-Tirmidhi 2165. Keep the contact purposeful, time-bound, and aimed at a decision. For more on staying within the bounds while getting to know a prospect, see our guide on the halal way to get to know someone for marriage.

Step 4: Involve the Wali From the Beginning

For a woman, the wali (guardian, normally her father) is not an optional courtesy. He is a condition of a valid nikah. The Prophet (peace be upon him) said there is no marriage without a wali, as narrated in Sunan Abu Dawud 2085, and that any woman who marries without the permission of her wali, her marriage is invalid, repeated three times, in Sunan al-Tirmidhi 1102.

This is a protection, not a restriction. The wali represents the woman's interests, vets the suitor, negotiates on her behalf, and shields her from being deceived or pressured. If a father is absent or refuses without valid Islamic reason, guardianship passes to the next male relative in line, and in some situations to the authority or a trusted imam. Do not treat the wali as a formality to be added at the signing. Bring him in at the start so the whole process is sound.

The man, for his part, approaches the woman's family directly and openly. There is no secret relationship that the families discover later. Honesty and transparency are the markers of a halal pursuit.

Step 5: Meet for the Purpose of Marriage

Islam does not ask you to marry a stranger blindly. It encourages you to look at and learn about a prospective spouse before committing. The Prophet (peace be upon him) advised a man who was getting engaged to look at her, as it is more likely to bring harmony between them, narrated in Sunan al-Tirmidhi 1087.

But this is done within limits. Any meeting should be in the presence of the wali or a mahram, not alone. The conversation is serious and to the point: ask about religious practice, character, expectations of marriage, family life, finances, where you will live, views on raising children, and anything that genuinely affects the decision. These are the questions that matter, and they are far more revealing than small talk.

Keep the number of meetings reasonable and the purpose clear. The aim is a decision, not a relationship. Once both sides are satisfied, you move to commitment rather than letting things linger.

Step 6: The Khitbah (Proposal and Engagement)

The khitbah is the formal proposal. The man (or his family) asks for the woman's hand, and if accepted, the two are engaged. Understand what engagement is and is not. It is a promise to marry. It does not make anything between them halal. The couple are still non-mahram to each other until the nikah is contracted. So the same boundaries apply: no seclusion, no physical contact, no behaving as though already married.

It is also forbidden to propose over the proposal of your brother once a prior proposal has been accepted, as the Prophet (peace be upon him) prohibited in Sahih al-Bukhari 5142. Once one suitor has been accepted, others step back.

Step 7: Agree the Mahr

The mahr is the gift the husband gives the wife, and it is her right and her property alone. Allah commands men to give women their mahr graciously, in Qur'an 4:4. It is a pillar of the marriage, not a tip to the family, and no one else has a claim to it.

The Sunnah is to keep it modest and not to compete over inflated amounts. The Prophet (peace be upon him) said the best marriage is the one that is easiest, as narrated in Sunan Abu Dawud 2117. He married companions for very small mahr, including teaching the Qur'an and a ring of iron, as in Sahih al-Bukhari 5135. The mahr should be real and agreed, but it should never become a barrier that delays or destroys good marriages. To understand the details, read our dedicated guide on the mahr and how to set it.

Step 8: The Nikah Contract

The nikah is the marriage itself. Its essential pillars are straightforward: the offer and acceptance (ijab and qabul) between the parties, the wali giving the woman in marriage, two trustworthy Muslim witnesses, and the mahr. With these in place, said clearly and consented to, the couple are married in the sight of Allah.

The contract is usually conducted by an imam or knowledgeable person who delivers a brief khutbah, confirms the consent of both parties, states the mahr, and has the witnesses observe. The woman's consent is required. A previously married woman must give explicit consent, and a virgin's permission must be sought, as the Prophet (peace be upon him) made clear in Sahih al-Bukhari 5136. She cannot be forced.

Keep the ceremony free of innovated rituals and cultural practices that contradict the Sunnah. Register the marriage with the civil authorities where you live as well, so your rights and your spouse's rights are documented and protected. For the full breakdown of conditions and clauses, see our guide on the nikah contract.

Step 9: The Walima

The walima is the marriage feast hosted by the husband to announce and celebrate the union. It is a confirmed Sunnah. The Prophet (peace be upon him) told Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf to hold a walima even with a single sheep, as narrated in Sahih al-Bukhari 5167. Announcing the marriage publicly is part of the deen, distinguishing the halal union from anything secret or doubtful.

The walima should match your means. It does not need to be extravagant, and going into debt to impress people is the opposite of the Sunnah. Keep it within the bounds of the Shariah: no free-mixing in a way that breeds fitnah, no music and conduct that displease Allah, no wastefulness. A modest, joyful gathering that feeds people, including the poor, is what was loved by the Prophet (peace be upon him). Accepting a walima invitation is itself encouraged, as in Sahih Muslim 1429.

A Note on Timing and Tawakkul

Do not let people frighten you out of marriage with talk of needing to be perfectly established first. Allah promises in Qur'an 24:32 that if the spouses are poor, He will enrich them from His bounty. Be responsible and prepared, yes. But marriage is a means of barakah and provision, not something to delay endlessly while chasing an imagined level of readiness. Frequently Asked Questions

Can I get married without a wali?

For a woman, no. The authentic hadith are explicit that there is no valid marriage without a wali, as in Sunan Abu Dawud 2085. If a father unjustly refuses, guardianship moves to the next eligible guardian or the Muslim authority. This is a condition of the nikah, not a custom that can be dropped.

Is it permissible to talk to a prospect before marriage?

Yes, for the serious purpose of deciding on marriage, and within boundaries. That means no seclusion, no flirtation, and ideally in the presence of the wali or a mahram. The questions should be about deen, character, and the practical realities of married life. Ongoing private "talking stages" with no commitment are not from the deen.

How much should the mahr be?

There is no fixed amount in the Shariah. It should be agreed, real, and given to the wife as her own property, as commanded in Qur'an 4:4. The Sunnah is to keep it modest and easy. Inflated demands that block marriage go against the prophetic guidance that the easiest marriage is the most blessed.

What actually makes a nikah valid?

The core pillars: an offer and acceptance between the parties, the woman's wali, two trustworthy Muslim witnesses, the mahr, and the genuine consent of both spouses. When these are present and stated clearly, the marriage is valid. Cultural extras are not conditions.

Is a walima obligatory?

It is a strongly confirmed Sunnah rather than a strict obligation, and it should be held according to your means, even something small as the Prophet (peace be upon him) instructed in Sahih al-Bukhari 5167. Announcing the marriage publicly is the important goal, not extravagance.

How long should the whole process take?

There is no fixed timeline, but the Islamic approach favours moving with purpose once you have found someone suitable and done your due diligence. Dragging an engagement out for years tends to create exactly the temptations the Shariah protects against. Decide, contract the nikah, and begin married life.
