Nikah Dua: The Authentic Supplications of a Muslim Marriage

﷽

In the name of Allah, the Most Merciful, the Bestower of Mercy.

We try to follow and explain from the Qur'an and the authentic Hadith, upon the understanding of the three rightly-guided generations (the Salaf as-Salih). All good is from Allah alone, and any mistake or shortcoming is from ourselves.

TL;DR: There is no single magic "nikah dua" that seals a marriage. What the Sunnah actually teaches is the Khutbat al-Hajah recited before the contract, the du'a of blessing said to the couple afterwards ("Barakallahu laka wa baraka 'alayka wa jama'a baynakuma fi khayr"), a du'a for goodness in the new spouse, and the du'a said before intimacy. All are established in authentic narrations, and we cover each one below.

Many people go looking for a special formula to recite "during the nikah" as if the words themselves carry a secret power. That is not how the deen works. A nikah is made valid by its pillars: the proposal and acceptance (ijab and qabul), the wali, the witnesses, and the mahr. The du'as connected to marriage are acts of worship and requests for barakah, not incantations. Knowing them, saying them with understanding, and meaning them is what matters.

Below are the supplications that are genuinely from the Qur'an and the authentic Sunnah, in the order they appear around a marriage.

What people mean by "nikah dua"

When people search for a nikah dua they usually want one of a few things: the sermon read before the contract, the blessing said to the bride and groom, a du'a to ask Allah for a righteous spouse, or the du'a said on the wedding night. The deen provides for all of these, but it does not provide a single sentence you recite to "activate" the marriage.

The contract itself is offer and acceptance in front of witnesses, with the woman's wali giving her in marriage and a mahr agreed upon. The Prophet, sallallahu 'alayhi wa sallam, said, "There is no nikah except with a wali," reported in Sunan Abi Dawud 2085 and graded authentic. That is the structure. The du'as sit around that structure to seek Allah's blessing on it.

Allah reminds us why marriage matters in the first place: "And of His signs is that He created for you from yourselves mates that you may find tranquillity in them; and He placed between you affection and mercy," Qur'an 30:21. Every du'a below is really a request for that tranquillity, affection, and mercy to take root.

The Khutbat al-Hajah: the sermon before the contract

The most established recitation connected to a nikah is not strictly a du'a but a khutbah, the "sermon of need," which the Prophet, sallallahu 'alayhi wa sallam, taught his companions and used to open important matters including marriage. It is reported in Sunan Abi Dawud 2118 and elsewhere with an authentic chain.

It opens with praise of Allah and seeking His help and forgiveness, then seeking refuge in Him from the evil of our own souls and our bad deeds. It then affirms that whoever Allah guides none can misguide, and whoever He leaves astray none can guide, followed by the testimony of faith.

The khutbah then joins three verses of the Qur'an:

  • "O mankind, fear your Lord, who created you from one soul..." Qur'an 4:1
  • "O you who have believed, fear Allah as He should be feared and do not die except as Muslims..." Qur'an 3:102
  • "O you who have believed, fear Allah and speak words of appropriate justice..." Qur'an 33:70

This is what is traditionally read by the one officiating before the ijab and qabul. It is a Sunnah, not a condition for validity. A nikah is still valid without it, but reviving it is a good thing and it sets the right tone: taqwa first, then the contract.

The du'a said to the newly married couple

This is the supplication most worth memorising, because you will say it every time someone you know gets married. When a man was congratulated on his marriage, the Prophet, sallallahu 'alayhi wa sallam, taught that he should be greeted with:

Barakallahu laka, wa baraka 'alayka, wa jama'a baynakuma fi khayr.

Meaning, roughly: "May Allah bless you, and send blessings upon you, and join you together in goodness." This is reported in Sunan Abi Dawud 2130 and Jami' at-Tirmidhi 1091, where it is graded hasan sahih.

Notice what it is not. It is not the pre-Islamic congratulation of "bir-rafa' wal-banin" (with harmony and sons), which the Prophet, sallallahu 'alayhi wa sallam, actually discouraged. The Sunnah greeting asks for barakah and for the couple to be gathered upon good, which covers far more than children alone. If you attend a walimah, this is the phrase to say to the groom.

The du'a for goodness in your spouse

The Sunnah also teaches a du'a for when you take a wife (and by extension the meaning applies to a woman entering marriage too), asking Allah for the good in her and seeking refuge from any harm. The Prophet, sallallahu 'alayhi wa sallam, instructed that a man take hold of his wife and say:

Allahumma inni as'aluka khayraha wa khayra ma jabaltaha 'alayh, wa a'udhu bika min sharriha wa sharri ma jabaltaha 'alayh.

Meaning: "O Allah, I ask You for the good in her and the good in the nature You have given her, and I seek refuge in You from her evil and the evil in the nature You have given her." This is reported in Sunan Abi Dawud 2160 and Sunan Ibn Majah 1918 and is graded hasan.

This du'a is a quiet lesson in realistic expectations. You are asking for the good and seeking protection from the bad in the same breath, because no spouse is without both. Marrying someone of sound deen and character, which is the standard the Prophet, sallallahu 'alayhi wa sallam, told us to prioritise in Sahih al-Bukhari 5090, is what tilts the balance towards the good. The du'a before intimacy

This one is important and often overlooked. Before a husband and wife are intimate, the Sunnah is to say:

Bismillah, Allahumma jannibna ash-shaytana wa jannib ish-shaytana ma razaqtana.

Meaning: "In the name of Allah. O Allah, keep the shaytan away from us, and keep the shaytan away from what You provide us." The Prophet, sallallahu 'alayhi wa sallam, said that if a child is decreed from that union, the shaytan will never harm that child. This is reported in Sahih al-Bukhari 141 and Sahih Muslim 1434.

It is a short, authentic du'a with a great reward attached, and it belongs to the marriage itself rather than the ceremony. Teach it to your spouse early so it becomes a habit from the first night.

Du'a for finding a righteous spouse

Before any of the above, many are still asking Allah to grant them a spouse at all. There is no specific fixed formula reported for this, and you should be wary of anyone selling one as "the" marriage du'a. Instead, use the du'as the Qur'an itself puts on the tongues of the believers.

The most fitting is the supplication of the righteous servants of the Most Merciful: "Our Lord, grant us from among our spouses and offspring comfort to our eyes, and make us leaders for the righteous," Qur'an 25:74. You can also make du'a in your own words, in your own language, in sujud and in the last third of the night, because sincere du'a needs no set script.

While you make du'a, take the means Allah commanded. Du'a and effort are two wings of the same bird. If you want a clear picture of the whole process from intention to contract, our guide on how to get married in Islam walks through it step by step.

Common mistakes around nikah du'a

A few things to avoid, because they are widespread:

  • Treating a du'a as a spell. No phrase makes a nikah valid or magically guarantees a happy marriage. The pillars make it valid; taqwa and effort make it thrive.
  • Cultural congratulations dressed as Sunnah. "Wishing sons and prosperity" is not the Prophetic greeting. Use "Barakallahu laka..." instead.
  • Weak or invented du'as passed around online. Many circulated "nikah du'as" have no authentic chain. Stick to what is confirmed in the reliable collections. If you cannot verify a narration, do not attribute it to the Prophet, sallallahu 'alayhi wa sallam. He warned sternly against lying upon him, as in Sahih al-Bukhari 110.
  • Neglecting the du'a before intimacy. It is authentic, easy, and carries real reward. Do not skip it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there one specific dua recited during the nikah ceremony?

No. The contract is completed by the proposal, acceptance, wali, witnesses, and mahr. What is recited is the Khutbat al-Hajah before the contract, and the du'a of blessing ("Barakallahu laka wa baraka 'alayka wa jama'a baynakuma fi khayr") is said to congratulate the couple afterwards. There is no single sentence that "performs" the nikah.

What do you say to congratulate a bride or groom?

Say "Barakallahu laka, wa baraka 'alayka, wa jama'a baynakuma fi khayr," which asks Allah to bless them and gather them upon good. It is reported in Sunan Abi Dawud 2130 and graded hasan sahih in Jami' at-Tirmidhi 1091.

What is the dua before intimacy on the wedding night?

"Bismillah, Allahumma jannibna ash-shaytana wa jannib ish-shaytana ma razaqtana," reported in Sahih al-Bukhari 141 and Sahih Muslim 1434. The Prophet, sallallahu 'alayhi wa sallam, said the shaytan will not harm any child decreed from that union.

Is a dua to find a spouse from the Sunnah?

There is no single fixed formula reported specifically for this. Use the Qur'anic du'a of Qur'an 25:74 and make sincere du'a in your own words at the times of acceptance, such as in sujud and the last third of the night, while also taking the lawful means to marry.

Does skipping the Khutbat al-Hajah invalidate a nikah?

No. The khutbah is a recommended Sunnah that sets the marriage on taqwa, but it is not a condition of validity. The nikah is valid once its pillars are met. Reviving the khutbah is still encouraged.

Where can I verify these duas myself?

Check the reliable collections directly through the links in this article and consult a qualified scholar for any detail you are unsure about. Avoid du'as circulated without a source, and never attribute an unverified narration to the Prophet, sallallahu 'alayhi wa sallam.
