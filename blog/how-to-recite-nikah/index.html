How To Recite Nikah: A Practical Guide

﷽

In the name of Allah, the Most Merciful, the Bestower of Mercy.

We try to follow and explain from the Qur'an and the authentic Hadith, upon the understanding of the three rightly-guided generations (the Salaf as-Salih). All good is from Allah alone, and any mistake or shortcoming is from ourselves.

TL;DR: The nikah is a contract, not a mystical formula. To "recite" it correctly you need the wali (the bride's guardian) to give her in marriage, two trustworthy Muslim witnesses present, an agreed mahr, and a clear offer (ijab) and acceptance (qabul). Many begin with the Prophetic khutbat al-hajah, then the wali says the words of giving and the groom says the words of accepting. That is the whole of it.

What "reciting the nikah" actually means

There is a common assumption that a nikah is valid only if a special string of Arabic is pronounced perfectly by an imam. That is not the case. The nikah in Islam is a solemn contract (aqd), and Allah calls it a weighty covenant: "and they have taken from you a solemn covenant" (Qur'an 4:21). What people call "reciting the nikah" is really the combination of the sermon that opens the gathering and the words of offer and acceptance that seal the contract.

So the honest answer to how to recite nikah is: get the pillars right, keep it simple, and follow the Sunnah rather than inherited custom. The Prophet, sallallahu alayhi wa sallam, and his Companions married with brief, dignified words and no theatre. For a fuller treatment of the document and its conditions, see our guide on the nikah contract.

The pillars that must be in place first

Before any words are spoken, the contract must rest on its foundations. If these are missing, no amount of beautiful recitation makes the marriage valid.

1. The wali (guardian) of the bride

A woman does not marry herself off. Her wali, normally her father, then the nearest male relative in order, contracts the marriage on her behalf with her consent. The Prophet, sallallahu alayhi wa sallam, said: "There is no marriage except with a guardian" (an authentic narration in Sunan Abi Dawud 2085, graded sahih). He also said: "Any woman who marries without the permission of her guardian, her marriage is invalid," repeated three times (Sunan al-Tirmidhi 1102, authentic). This is not a formality to be argued away. We explain the guardian's role in detail in our guide on the wali in Islam.

2. Two trustworthy witnesses

The contract must be witnessed. This is what separates a lawful marriage from a secret arrangement. The scholars derived from the Sunnah that a valid nikah requires witnesses, so that the union is announced and known, not hidden. Two upright Muslim men serve as the witnesses to the offer and acceptance.

3. Consent of both parties

A marriage forced on an unwilling woman is defective. A woman came to the Prophet, sallallahu alayhi wa sallam, saying her father had married her against her will, and he gave her the choice (Sunan Ibn Majah 1875, authentic). The wali speaks the words, but the bride's agreement must be real.

4. The mahr

The mahr is the bridal gift the husband gives to the wife. Allah commands: "And give the women their bridal gifts graciously" (Qur'an 4:4). It is her right and her property, not the family's. More on this below.

The khutbat al-hajah: how the gathering opens

It is from the Sunnah to begin the nikah with the khutbat al-hajah, the sermon the Prophet, sallallahu alayhi wa sallam, taught his Companions to use before matters of importance. Abdullah ibn Mas'ud narrated it, and it is recorded in Sunan Abi Dawud 2118 and Sunan al-Tirmidhi 1105 as an authentic narration.

It begins:

"Inna al-hamda lillahi, nahmaduhu wa nasta'inuhu wa nastaghfiruh, wa na'udhu billahi min shururi anfusina wa sayyi'ati a'malina. Man yahdihi Allahu fala mudilla lah, wa man yudlil fala hadiya lah. Wa ashhadu an la ilaha illa Allah, wa ashhadu anna Muhammadan abduhu wa rasuluh."

The meaning: All praise is due to Allah. We praise Him, seek His help, and seek His forgiveness. We seek refuge in Allah from the evil of ourselves and from our bad deeds. Whoever Allah guides, none can misguide, and whoever He leaves astray, none can guide. I bear witness that there is no god but Allah, and I bear witness that Muhammad is His servant and Messenger.

This is then followed by three verses of the Qur'an: Qur'an 3:102, Qur'an 4:1, and Qur'an 33:70-71. These verses all call to taqwa of Allah, which sets the right tone for a marriage built to please Him. The one giving the sermon, whether the wali, the groom, or an imam invited to do so, recites this, then moves to the contract itself.

A note on measure: the khutbat al-hajah is a recommended Sunnah, not a condition of validity. A nikah without it is still valid. But it is a beautiful and easy Sunnah to revive, and it displaces the cultural rituals that have crept into wedding gatherings with no basis in the deen.

The ijab and qabul: the words that seal the contract

This is the heart of the matter. The contract is concluded by an offer (ijab) from the wali and an acceptance (qabul) from the groom, in the presence of the witnesses.

The wali says, in effect:

"I marry you my daughter [name] for the mahr of [state the mahr]."

The groom replies:

"I accept her marriage to myself for this mahr."

That is enough. The witnesses hear both statements, and the marriage is contracted. It does not need to be in classical Arabic; the words are valid in any language the two parties understand, because the point is a clear, unambiguous offer and acceptance. What matters is that the meaning is definite: this man is now married to this woman, with this mahr, before these witnesses.

The simplicity here is deliberate. The Prophet, sallallahu alayhi wa sallam, married a man to a woman for a mahr as small as an iron ring, and in one case for what the man had memorised of the Qur'an, saying: "I have given her to you in marriage for what you know of the Qur'an" (Sahih al-Bukhari 5087 and Sahih Muslim 1425). No lengthy liturgy was recited. The contract was concluded with clear words and honest intent. For the wider picture of the process, from proposal to marriage, see how to get married in Islam.

Where the mahr fits in the recitation

The mahr is named aloud during the contract, so the witnesses know its amount and the wife knows her right. It can be money, gold, a fixed benefit, or, as the hadith above shows, teaching the Qur'an. There is no minimum that scholars all agree upon beyond that it be something of real value, but the guiding principle is ease. Marriages should not be weighed down with crushing sums that delay righteous unions. If part of the mahr is deferred, that too is stated openly so there is no dispute later. We cover amounts, deferral, and rights in the guide to the mahr.

Common mistakes and cultural additions to avoid

A few things regularly attach themselves to the nikah that are either unnecessary or contrary to the Sunnah:

  • Treating an imam as a requirement. No imam is needed for a valid nikah. The wali, groom, and witnesses are the essential parties. An imam is often invited to give the khutbah and organise the gathering, which is fine, but his presence is not a pillar.
  • Insisting on flawless Arabic. The offer and acceptance are valid in the language the parties speak. Do not let fear of mispronunciation paralyse a straightforward contract.
  • Inflated mahr and lavish expense. Ease was the Prophetic way. Burdening the groom's family to satisfy custom pushes young people away from marriage and towards sin.
  • Sidelining the bride's consent. The wali speaks, but her genuine agreement is required. Pressuring or coercing her corrupts the contract.
  • Innovated group recitations and rituals. Anything presented as a religiously required chant or ceremony beyond the Sunnah risks bid'ah. Keep to what is transmitted.

A lawful nikah begins long before the words are spoken, with a serious search for a spouse of deen and character. The Prophet, sallallahu alayhi wa sallam, directed the believer to "choose the one of religious commitment" (Sahih al-Bukhari 5090), and Allah describes marriage as a source of tranquillity, love, and mercy (Qur'an 30:21). Frequently Asked Questions

Is there a fixed Arabic formula I must recite for the nikah to count?

No. There is no single fixed formula whose exact wording is a condition of validity. What is required is a clear offer from the wali and a clear acceptance from the groom, before two witnesses, with an agreed mahr. The khutbat al-hajah is a recommended Sunnah to open with, but the contract stands on the offer and acceptance.

Can the nikah be recited in English or another language?

Yes. The offer and acceptance are valid in any language both parties understand, because the objective is an unambiguous contract. Reciting the khutbat al-hajah in Arabic is preferred, since it is the transmitted wording, but the meaning may be explained afterwards for those who do not know Arabic.

Does an imam have to perform the nikah?

No. The essential parties are the wali of the bride, the groom, and two trustworthy Muslim witnesses. An imam is commonly asked to deliver the sermon and lead the gathering, which is permissible and orderly, but his presence is not a pillar of the marriage.

What if the woman has no father to act as wali?

Guardianship then passes to the nearest male relatives in order, such as the paternal grandfather, then brothers, then paternal uncles. If a woman has no relative who can serve, the authority of the Muslim community, such as a trusted imam or judge, acts as her wali, based on the hadith: "the ruler is the guardian of the one who has no guardian" (Sunan Abi Dawud 2083, authentic). She still does not marry herself off without one.

How small can the mahr be?

It must be something of genuine value, but the Sunnah strongly encourages keeping it easy. The Prophet, sallallahu alayhi wa sallam, accepted an iron ring and even teaching the Qur'an as mahr (Sahih al-Bukhari 5087). Modesty in the mahr is praiseworthy, not shameful.

Is the khutbat al-hajah required for the nikah to be valid?

No. It is a recommended Sunnah that the Prophet, sallallahu alayhi wa sallam, taught, and reviving it is good. But a nikah concluded with only the offer, acceptance, witnesses, and mahr is valid even without it.
