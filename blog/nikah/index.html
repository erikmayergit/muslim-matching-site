Nikah

﷽

In the name of Allah, the Most Merciful, the Bestower of Mercy.

We try to follow and explain from the Qur'an and the authentic Hadith, upon the understanding of the three rightly-guided generations (the Salaf as-Salih). All good is from Allah alone, and any mistake or shortcoming is from ourselves.

TL;DR: Nikah is the Islamic marriage contract, an act of worship that Allah made lawful between a man and a woman. It becomes valid through the woman's guardian (walī), her consent, two witnesses, an offer and acceptance, and a mahr given to the bride. Done correctly, it is half of a believer's deen and the only lawful path to companionship in Islam.

What Nikah Means in Islam

Nikah is the marriage contract that Allah legislated to join a man and a woman in a lawful, honourable bond. It is not merely a social arrangement or a romantic milestone. It is an act of worship, a Sunnah of the Prophets, and the door through which what was forbidden between two strangers becomes something rewarded.

Allah made this bond a sign of His mercy. He said: "And among His signs is that He created for you mates from among yourselves, that you may find tranquillity in them, and He placed between you affection and mercy" (Qur'an 30:21). Marriage in Islam is built on sakīnah, tranquillity, and on mercy, not on desire alone.

The Prophet, salla Allahu 'alayhi wa sallam, tied nikah directly to a person's religion. It is reported that he said: "Whoever marries has completed half of his religion, so let him fear Allah regarding the other half" (an authentic narration; see the meaning affirmed by scholars of hadith). And he was clear that marriage is his own path: "Marriage is part of my Sunnah, and whoever turns away from my Sunnah is not from me" (Sahih al-Bukhari 5063).

This is why we treat nikah as a matter of the deen first, not a matter of feelings first. If you want the full picture of how a marriage comes together from start to finish, see our guide on how to get married in Islam.

Why Nikah Is Encouraged, and For Whom

Allah commands the believers to marry off the single among them: "And marry the unmarried among you and the righteous among your male and female slaves. If they should be poor, Allah will enrich them from His bounty" (Qur'an 24:32). Poverty is not an excuse to delay marriage indefinitely, because provision is in the hand of Allah.

The Prophet, salla Allahu 'alayhi wa sallam, addressed young people directly: "O young people, whoever among you is able to marry, let him marry, for it restrains the gaze and guards the private parts. And whoever is not able, let him fast, for it will be a shield for him" (Sahih al-Bukhari 5065). Marriage here is medicine for the lower self and a protection from fitnah.

For a person who fears falling into the haram and has the means to marry, the scholars hold that nikah becomes strongly obligated upon him. For others it remains a highly recommended Sunnah. In every case, the point stands: Islam pushes the believer toward marriage and away from the lonely, exposed condition that leads to sin.

The Pillars and Conditions of a Valid Nikah

A nikah is not valid just because two people agree to it. Allah gave it a structure, and that structure protects the woman, the family, and the wider society. The following elements must be present.

1. The two parties, free of impediments

There must be a man and a woman who are lawful for each other to marry. This rules out those Allah forbade in Qur'an 4:23, such as close blood relatives and certain relations through marriage and breastfeeding. Both must consent; a marriage forced on an unwilling person is defective.

2. The walī (the woman's guardian)

The walī is not an optional formality. A woman does not contract her own nikah alone. Her guardian, normally her father, then her grandfather, then a close male relative in order, gives her in marriage. The Prophet, salla Allahu 'alayhi wa sallam, said: "There is no marriage without a walī" (Sunan Abi Dawud 2085, authentic).

He was even clearer: "Any woman who marries without the permission of her walī, her marriage is invalid, her marriage is invalid, her marriage is invalid" (Jami' at-Tirmidhi 1102, authentic). This is the position supported by the strongest evidence upon the understanding of the Salaf, and no modern discomfort with it changes the text. The walī is a shield for the woman, not a leash. His job is to secure her interest and refuse a man who is not good for her deen or her life. We cover this role in detail in our guide to the role of the wali.

3. The consent of the woman

The walī cannot force a woman into a marriage she rejects. The Prophet, salla Allahu 'alayhi wa sallam, said a previously married woman must not be married until she is consulted, and a virgin must not be married until her permission is sought (Sahih al-Bukhari 5136). When a woman came to him complaining that her father married her against her wishes, he gave her the choice to keep or annul the marriage (reported in Sunan Abi Dawud, authentic). Consent and guardianship work together; neither cancels the other.

4. Two witnesses

A nikah must be witnessed. It is a public, announced commitment, not a secret arrangement between a couple. The scholars derive from the Sunnah that a valid contract requires the presence of witnesses, and the Prophet, salla Allahu 'alayhi wa sallam, ordered that marriage be announced. Secrecy is the mark of something shameful; nikah is the opposite of that.

5. The offer and acceptance (ījāb wa qabūl)

The contract is concluded by a clear offer from the walī and a clear acceptance from the husband, in the same sitting, before the witnesses. This is the moment the two become husband and wife. There is no need for elaborate wording; clarity and intent are what matter.

6. The mahr

The mahr is the payment the husband gives to the wife as her right. Allah commanded: "And give the women their dowries graciously" (Qur'an 4:4). It belongs to her alone, not to her father or her family. It can be large or modest. The Prophet, salla Allahu 'alayhi wa sallam, told a man who had nothing to offer even an iron ring to give what he could, and in that case he married him to a woman on the condition that he teach her what he knew of the Qur'an (Sahih al-Bukhari 5087). Do not let inflated cultural demands turn the mahr into a barrier. The best marriage is the one that is easiest. For more on this, see our guide to the nikah contract and mahr.

What Nikah Is Not

Because the deen has clear limits, it is worth naming what falls outside them. Nikah is not a private romance that two people build in secret and then formalise later. There is no legitimate "talking stage," no dating, and no unsupervised getting-to-know-you outside the boundaries Allah set. Allah commanded the believers to lower their gaze and guard their chastity (Qur'an 24:30), and the Prophet, salla Allahu 'alayhi wa sallam, warned that no man is alone with a woman except that Shaytan is the third of them (reported in Jami' at-Tirmidhi, authentic).

Getting to know a prospective spouse is legitimate and encouraged, but it happens through the walī, with clear intention, and within modest limits, not through months of private messaging that erode the deen before the contract even exists. Choosing a Spouse: The Criterion That Matters

Once you understand that nikah is worship, the question of whom to marry answers itself. The Prophet, salla Allahu 'alayhi wa sallam, said: "A woman is married for four things: her wealth, her lineage, her beauty, and her religion. So choose the one with religion, may your hands be rubbed with dust" (Sahih al-Bukhari 5090). The same standard applies to the man a woman accepts.

He also instructed guardians directly: "If there comes to you one whose religion and character please you, then marry him. If you do not, there will be fitnah on the earth and widespread corruption" (Jami' at-Tirmidhi 1084, authentic). Deen and character come first. Everything else is secondary, and a marriage that starts on wealth or worldly appeal without taqwa is building on sand.

After the Contract: The Walīmah

The walīmah is the marriage feast, a confirmed Sunnah after the nikah. When 'Abd al-Rahman ibn 'Awf married, the Prophet, salla Allahu 'alayhi wa sallam, told him: "Give a feast, even with one sheep" (Sahih al-Bukhari 5167). The walīmah announces the marriage publicly and shares joy with the community. It does not need to be extravagant, and drowning a young couple in debt for a lavish celebration contradicts the very ease Islam encourages.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a nikah valid without a walī?

No. The authentic Sunnah is explicit: "There is no marriage without a walī" (Sunan Abi Dawud 2085), and a woman who marries without her guardian's permission has an invalid marriage (Jami' at-Tirmidhi 1102). This is the position on the strongest evidence. If a guardian is unjustly refusing every suitable, righteous man, that is a matter to take to a knowledgeable Islamic authority, not something a woman resolves by contracting a nikah on her own.

Is a mahr required, and how much should it be?

The mahr is the wife's binding right (Qur'an 4:4) and no nikah should be treated as complete without it being agreed. There is no fixed minimum in the Sunnah; the Prophet, salla Allahu 'alayhi wa sallam, accepted very small amounts. The guidance is ease, not extravagance. It belongs to the wife alone.

Does the woman have to agree to the marriage?

Yes. A woman cannot be married against her will. Her permission must be sought, and a previously married woman's clear word and a virgin's assent are both required (Sahih al-Bukhari 5136). The walī secures her interest; he does not override her refusal.

Can we get to know each other before the nikah?

Yes, within the limits of the Shariah. That means an honest, purposeful process through the walī, without seclusion (khalwah) and without the private, drawn-out "talking" that the culture normalises. Ask serious questions about deen, character, and life, with the guardian aware and involved.

What actually makes the nikah happen?

The contract is concluded by the offer from the walī and the acceptance from the husband, in one sitting, before two witnesses, with the mahr agreed and both parties consenting. At that moment they are lawfully married.

Is marriage really half of the religion?

The Prophet, salla Allahu 'alayhi wa sallam, described marriage as completing half of a person's deen and urged the believer to fear Allah in the remaining half. Understood correctly, this shows how much protection and reward Allah placed in a lawful marriage, and how seriously the believer should take entering it.
