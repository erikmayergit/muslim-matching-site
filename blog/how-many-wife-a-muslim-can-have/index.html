How Many Wives Can a Muslim Man Have in Islam?

﷽

In the name of Allah, the Most Merciful, the Bestower of Mercy.

We try to follow and explain from the Qur'an and the authentic Hadith, upon the understanding of the three rightly-guided generations (the Salaf as-Salih). All good is from Allah alone, and any mistake or shortcoming is from ourselves.

TL;DR: A Muslim man may marry up to four wives at one time, and no more. This permission comes with a heavy condition: he must deal justly between them in maintenance, time, and treatment. A woman has one husband. If a man fears he cannot be just, Allah commands him to keep to one.

The Direct Answer From the Qur'an

The question of how many wives a Muslim can have is answered plainly in the Book of Allah. Allah says:

"...marry those that please you of women, two or three or four. But if you fear that you will not be just, then only one..." Qur'an 4:3

The ceiling is four. This is not a matter of scholarly dispute. When Ghaylan ibn Salamah accepted Islam and had ten wives from the time of his disbelief, the Prophet (peace be upon him) ordered him to keep four and part from the rest, reported in Sunan Ibn Majah 1953 and graded authentic. So the maximum for a man at any one time is four wives, and marrying a fifth while still married to four is not permitted.

A woman, by contrast, is married to one man at a time. This is not an oversight or an imbalance to be argued away. It is the order Allah legislated, tied to certainty of lineage and the distinct roles He assigned. We take it as it came, without apology.

Permission, Not a Command

Polygyny in Islam is permitted (mubah), not obligatory and not recommended for its own sake. A man who marries once and stays with one wife his whole life has done nothing wrong. A man who marries more than one, meeting the conditions, has also done nothing wrong. The Prophet (peace be upon him) himself had more than one wife, and this is part of the Sunnah that Allah permitted.

So this is not something to boast about, nor something to be ashamed of. It is a door Allah left open for real human situations: the imbalance between the number of men and women, widowhood, and the protection of chastity for both parties. What matters is the intention behind it and whether a man can carry the responsibility honestly.

The Central Condition: Justice

The permission in Qur'an 4:3 is fenced by a single word that changes everything: justice. Allah did not say "marry up to four and do as you please." He said marry more than one only if you can be just, and if you fear you cannot, keep to one.

The justice required here is in the things a man can control:

Maintenance and provision

Each wife has a right to housing, food, clothing, and support according to his means. A man who cannot financially carry two households has no business taking a second wife. Allah tied the marriage to a man's capability, and the Prophet (peace be upon him) told the youth to marry only when they had the means, reported in Sahih al-Bukhari 5066.

Division of time and nights

A man must divide his nights fairly between his wives. He cannot lavish one and abandon the other. The Prophet (peace be upon him) was scrupulous in this, dividing his time among his wives with fairness. When he intended to travel, he would draw lots among them, reported in Sahih al-Bukhari 2593.

Fair treatment and dignity

Beyond money and nights, each wife has a right to kind treatment, to not be humiliated, and to not be treated as a lesser arrangement. The stern warning here is severe:

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said: "Whoever has two wives and leans towards one of them, will come on the Day of Resurrection with one of his sides drooping." Reported in Sunan Abu Dawud 2133, Sunan an-Nasa'i 3942, and graded authentic.

That is a man arriving before Allah physically deformed because he tilted the scales in this life. It should sober anyone who treats a second marriage as a casual want rather than a weighty trust.

The Justice a Man Is Not Required to Give

There is a second verse that people sometimes throw at the whole institution, thinking it cancels the first. Allah says:

"And you will never be able to be equal between wives, even if you should strive to do so..." Qur'an 4:129

Some claim this proves polygyny is effectively forbidden, since perfect justice is impossible. That reading is wrong and contradicts the Sunnah, in which the Prophet and his Companions practised polygyny openly.

The scholars of tafsir explain that the justice a man cannot achieve, mentioned in Qur'an 4:129, is the inclination of the heart. A man cannot force his heart to feel identically toward two people. That is beyond human power, so Allah does not hold him accountable for it. The Prophet (peace be upon him) is reported to have divided fairly between his wives and then said, regarding the love in his heart, "O Allah, this is my division in what I control, so do not blame me for what You control and I do not," reported in Sunan Abu Dawud 2134. Scholars have discussed its chain, but its meaning is upheld across the sound evidence.

So the two verses fit together cleanly. Justice in actions (provision, nights, treatment) is required. Equality of feeling in the heart is not, because it lies outside a man's control. What a man must never do is let that inner inclination spill over into unfair treatment, which is exactly what Qur'an 4:129 warns against when it continues: "...so do not incline completely and leave her hanging."

Practical Conditions Before Taking a Second Wife

Beyond the two Qur'anic pillars of a maximum of four and the duty of justice, a man honestly assessing himself should weigh several things:

  • Financial capacity. Can he actually provide two separate, dignified households? Not a fantasy budget, a real one.
  • Ability to divide time. Does his work and life allow him to give each wife her right of nights and presence?
  • Fear of Allah over desire. Is the driving motive the fear of falling into haram, the desire to build a righteous household, or simply appetite dressed up in religious language?
  • Justice as a settled habit. A man who is already unjust, harsh, or negligent with one wife will not suddenly become just with two.

Islam does not require a man to seek the first wife's permission to marry again, and framing it as forbidden without her consent is not the ruling of the Shariah. That said, honesty, wisdom, and good character matter enormously in how a man handles this, and a God-fearing man does not use a permission of Allah as a weapon to wound the people he is responsible for.

Why This Law Is a Mercy, Not a Flaw

Critics of Islam often single out this ruling, so it is worth stating the wisdom plainly rather than shrinking from it. Allah reminds us in the same passage that this legislation protects orphans and vulnerable women Qur'an 4:3. Across history and even today, women outnumber men in many societies, especially after war and hardship. A system that limits every man to one wife and pretends the surplus does not exist pushes women toward either lifelong loneliness or illicit relationships with no rights, no contract, and no protection.

Islam's answer is a lawful, contracted marriage with defined rights, a mahr, maintenance, and the protection of a wali for the woman. That is dignity, not degradation. It keeps relationships inside the halal and inside accountability. Compare that honestly to a culture that forbids a second wife on paper while tolerating mistresses and affairs in practice, and the mercy of the Shariah becomes obvious.

At the same time, we do not oversell it. It is a demanding responsibility that many men are not equipped for, which is precisely why Allah closed the verse with "then only one." The default for a man who fears injustice is a single marriage.

Choosing on the Basis of Deen

Whether a man marries once or more than once, the criterion the Prophet (peace be upon him) set is the same. He said a woman is married for four things, and he instructed: "...so choose the one with deen, may your hands be rubbed with dust," reported in Sahih al-Bukhari 5090. The same standard applies to a woman assessing a suitor: his religion and character come first.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a Muslim man have more than four wives?

No. The limit of four is fixed by Qur'an 4:3 and confirmed by the Prophet's order to Ghaylan ibn Salamah to keep only four of his ten wives, in Sunan Ibn Majah 1953. No scholar upon the Sunnah permits a fifth simultaneous wife.

Does a man need his first wife's permission to marry a second?

Her permission is not a legal condition for the validity of the second marriage. However, honesty, wisdom, and kind treatment are obligations, and a righteous man handles the matter with fear of Allah rather than deceit or cruelty. For a specific situation, consult a qualified scholar.

Is polygyny recommended or just allowed?

It is permitted (mubah), not obligatory. A man who marries one wife for life has done nothing blameworthy. The permission exists for real needs, and it carries real conditions.

What does justice between wives actually mean?

Justice in what a man controls: provision, housing, division of nights, and fair treatment. Equality of the heart's love is not required, because it is beyond human control, as Qur'an 4:129 makes clear. What is forbidden is letting that inclination cause unfair treatment.

Can a woman have more than one husband?

No. A woman is married to one man at a time. This is established by consensus and tied to the certainty of lineage and the roles Allah assigned. It is not open to reinterpretation.

What happens to a man who is unjust between his wives?

The warning is severe. The Prophet (peace be upon him) said such a man will come on the Day of Resurrection with one side of his body drooping, reported in Sunan Abu Dawud 2133. Injustice between wives is a sin a man will answer for before Allah.
