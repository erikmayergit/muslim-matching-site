The Sunnah of Nikah: How Marriage Was Meant to Be

﷽

In the name of Allah, the Most Merciful, the Bestower of Mercy.

We try to follow and explain from the Qur'an and the authentic Hadith, upon the understanding of the three rightly-guided generations (the Salaf as-Salih). All good is from Allah alone, and any mistake or shortcoming is from ourselves.

TL;DR: The sunnah of nikah is to marry with the right intention, prioritise deen and character in a spouse, involve the woman's wali, agree an honest mahr, keep the whole affair simple and free of extravagance, and celebrate with a walimah. The Prophet (peace be upon him) said marriage is his way, and turning away from it is turning away from him.

Marriage in Islam is not a cultural formality or a romantic project borrowed from other nations. It is an act of worship with a clear structure, and the Prophet (peace be upon him) left us a complete pattern for it. When people say they want a wedding "according to the sunnah," they often mean the ceremony. But the sunnah of nikah begins long before the contract is signed, in the intention of the heart and the standard by which a spouse is chosen.

This guide walks through what the Qur'an and authentic Sunnah actually teach about nikah, so you can build a marriage on the same foundation the first generations did, not on customs that crept in later and got mistaken for religion.

Nikah Is an Act of Worship, Not Optional Custom

Allah placed marriage among His signs. He said that He created spouses for one another so that hearts find rest in each other, and He placed between them love and mercy (Qur'an 30:21). He also commanded the community to marry those who are single among them (Qur'an 24:32). This is not presented as a private lifestyle choice. It is an encouraged path with divine backing.

The Prophet (peace be upon him) made this explicit. When a group of men spoke of avoiding marriage to devote themselves to worship, he corrected them plainly, saying that he prays and sleeps, fasts and breaks his fast, and marries women, then adding that whoever turns away from his sunnah is not of him (Sahih al-Bukhari 5063). Marriage is therefore not a distraction from the deen. It is part of it.

He also addressed young people directly, telling them that whoever among them is able to marry should do so, because it lowers the gaze and guards chastity, and whoever cannot should fast (Sahih al-Bukhari 5065, also Sahih Muslim 1400). So the first pillar of the sunnah of nikah is simply the intention: to marry in order to obey Allah and protect oneself, not merely to satisfy a want.

Choosing a Spouse: Deen Comes First

The most well-known guidance on selection is the hadith that a woman is married for four things: her wealth, her lineage, her beauty, and her religion, and the Prophet (peace be upon him) instructed to choose the one of religion or your hands will be in the dust (Sahih al-Bukhari 5090, also Sahih Muslim 1466). The same principle applies to a woman choosing a husband. The Prophet (peace be upon him) taught that when a man whose religion and character please you proposes, he should be married, otherwise there will be corruption and widespread trouble (Sunan al-Tirmidhi 1084).

This is the wedge that separates a serious search from an idle one. Wealth fades, lineage does not feed a marriage, and worldly attractions do not survive hardship. Religion and character do. A person who fears Allah will treat a spouse justly whether things are easy or hard.

Allah also drew the boundary of who may be married: believing men and women, not those who reject faith (Qur'an 2:221). This is why a genuine, deen-first search matters from the very first step. Getting to Know Someone Without Crossing the Lines

The sunnah permits a man to look at the woman he intends to propose to, and to seek knowledge of her character and religion through legitimate means. What it does not permit is private seclusion, unchaperoned meetings, or an open-ended "talking stage" that mimics dating. The Prophet (peace be upon him) warned that no man is ever alone with a woman except that the third of them is Shaytan (Sunan al-Tirmidhi 2165). Serious enquiry is encouraged. Free-mixing dressed up as compatibility testing is not.

The Wali: A Condition of a Valid Nikah

A woman does not marry herself off. Her guardian, the wali, contracts the marriage on her behalf with her consent. This is not a cultural leftover. It is a condition established by the Sunnah. The Prophet (peace be upon him) said there is no marriage without a wali (Sunan Abi Dawud 2085, graded authentic). He also said that any woman who marries without the permission of her wali, her marriage is invalid, repeating it for emphasis (Sunan Abi Dawud 2083, also Sunan al-Tirmidhi 1102).

This protects the woman, it does not diminish her. Her consent is required, and a father cannot force a daughter into a marriage she rejects. But the contract runs through her guardian. Any framing that tells a Muslim woman she can bypass her wali to make herself independent is contradicting an explicit text, and it should be rejected. If a wali is unjustly refusing every suitable proposal without a valid reason, the matter is referred to a scholar or the Islamic authority, not resolved by the woman acting alone. The Mahr: A Right, Not a Price Tag

Allah commanded that women be given their mahr as an obligation and a free gift (Qur'an 4:4). The mahr is the wife's right, owned by her alone, not by her father or family. It is a pillar of the marriage, but the sunnah is that it be reasonable, not a barrier.

When a man came wanting to marry and had almost nothing, the Prophet (peace be upon him) told him to look for something to give, even a ring of iron, and when he had nothing at all, he married him on the basis of what he had memorised of the Qur'an (Sahih al-Bukhari 5087, also Sahih Muslim 1425). The lesson is direct. A modest, honest mahr that the man can actually pay is far closer to the sunnah than an inflated sum demanded for status. Turning mahr into a competition or a debt trap is a cultural distortion, not the religion.

Keep It Simple: The Sunnah Is Ease, Not Extravagance

The overwhelming spirit of the Prophetic marriage was simplicity. The contract is a few honest words witnessed properly. The Prophet (peace be upon him) described the offer and acceptance in front of witnesses, with the wali and the mahr, and that is the substance of it. He warned against being burdened by cost.

Many of the exhausting rituals that dominate weddings today have no basis in the Sunnah. Mixed-gender celebrations, music and immodesty, ruinous spending to impress people, drawn-out ceremonies rooted in other cultures: none of this is the sunnah of nikah, and some of it is outright haram. Announcing the marriage, on the other hand, is encouraged. The Prophet (peace be upon him) instructed that marriage be publicised (Sunan al-Tirmidhi 1089). A nikah should be known, not secret, but known without sin. For the fuller step-by-step, our guide on how to get married in Islam lays out the sequence.

The Walimah: The Sunnah Wedding Feast

After the marriage, the sunnah is to hold a walimah, a wedding feast. When Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf married, the Prophet (peace be upon him) told him to hold a walimah even if only with one sheep (Sahih al-Bukhari 5167). Notice again the theme: do something, but keep it within your means.

Accepting the invitation to a walimah is a duty upon the one invited, unless there is a valid excuse. The Prophet (peace be upon him) said that when one of you is invited to a walimah he should attend (Sahih al-Bukhari 5173, also Sahih Muslim 1429). He also criticised a walimah where only the rich are invited and the poor are left out. The feast is meant to spread joy and gratitude, not to display wealth. And like the rest of the celebration, it must stay free of the haram: no free-mixing beyond what the Shariah allows, no alcohol, no immodesty.

The Du'as of Nikah

The sunnah includes specific supplications around marriage, and using them brings barakah where invented rituals bring nothing.

When congratulating the couple, the reported du'a is to say, in meaning, may Allah bless you, and send blessings upon you, and join you both in goodness (Sunan al-Tirmidhi 1091, also Sunan Abi Dawud 2130). This is the sunnah greeting, not the borrowed phrases of other nations.

Before intimacy, the Prophet (peace be upon him) taught the du'a which means: in the name of Allah, O Allah keep the Shaytan away from us and keep the Shaytan away from what You grant us. He said that if a child is decreed between them, Shaytan will never harm it (Sahih al-Bukhari 141, also Sahih Muslim 1434). Small acts of remembrance like these turn a marriage into worship from its very first night.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is marriage fard or sunnah in Islam?

For most people it is a strongly encouraged sunnah, and the Prophet (peace be upon him) called it his way (Sahih al-Bukhari 5063). For a person who genuinely fears falling into the haram and is able to marry, scholars hold it becomes obligatory, because guarding one's chastity is a duty. The ruling shifts with a person's circumstances, so ask a qualified scholar about your own situation.

Can a woman marry without her wali?

No. The authentic Sunnah is clear that there is no valid nikah without a wali (Sunan Abi Dawud 2085). Her consent is required and she cannot be forced, but the contract is concluded through her guardian. Any teaching that removes the wali to fit modern norms contradicts an explicit hadith and should not be followed.

How much should the mahr be?

There is no fixed minimum beyond something of value, and the sunnah is ease. The Prophet (peace be upon him) accepted a ring of iron and even memorised Qur'an as mahr when a man had nothing (Sahih al-Bukhari 5087). Agree on an honest amount the husband can genuinely pay. Inflating the mahr for prestige is against the spirit of the Sunnah.

Is a walimah compulsory?

The walimah is a confirmed sunnah, and holding one, even modestly, follows the Prophet's instruction to Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf (Sahih al-Bukhari 5167). Attending when invited is a duty unless you have a valid excuse (Sahih Muslim 1429). It should be kept free of anything haram such as free-mixing or immodesty.

What makes a nikah valid?

In summary: the consent of both parties, the woman's wali, two witnesses, and a mahr, with a clear offer and acceptance. The Prophet (peace be upon him) also ordered that the marriage be announced (Sunan al-Tirmidhi 1089). For a detailed breakdown of each condition, see our guide to the nikah contract.
