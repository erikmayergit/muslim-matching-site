What Is Islam? A Clear Introduction to the Deen

﷽

In the name of Allah, the Most Merciful, the Bestower of Mercy.

We try to follow and explain from the Qur'an and the authentic Hadith, upon the understanding of the three rightly-guided generations (the Salaf as-Salih). All good is from Allah alone, and any mistake or shortcoming is from ourselves.

TL;DR: Islam is the religion of submitting to Allah alone, worshipping Him without partners, and following the final Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him). Allah says the only accepted religion with Him is Islam in Qur'an 3:19. It rests on sincere belief (iman), five acts of worship, and upright character, all drawn from the Qur'an and the authentic Sunnah.

The Meaning of Islam

The Arabic word Islam means submission and surrender to Allah, along with the peace and security that come from that surrender. A Muslim is the one who submits his will to Allah, obeys His commands, and keeps away from what He forbade.

This is not a new religion invented in the seventh century. It is the same message every prophet brought, from Adam to Nuh, Ibrahim, Musa, Isa, and finally Muhammad (peace be upon them all). Each called their people to worship Allah alone. Allah describes Ibrahim as a Muslim in meaning, saying he was neither Jew nor Christian but a true monotheist who submitted to Allah (Qur'an 3:67).

The purpose behind all of this is stated plainly. Allah says He did not create jinn and mankind except to worship Him (Qur'an 51:56). Islam, then, is the answer to why we exist. It tells a person who his Lord is, why he is here, and where he is going.

Tawhid: The Foundation of Everything

At the heart of Islam is tawhid, the absolute oneness of Allah. This is the first thing every prophet taught and the first thing a person must understand. Allah is one, without partner, without equal, and without any share in His lordship or His right to be worshipped.

Surah al-Ikhlas sums it up in four short verses: say He is Allah, One; Allah, the Self-Sufficient; He did not give birth, nor was He born; and there is none comparable to Him (Qur'an 112:1-4). The Prophet (peace be upon him) taught that this surah equals a third of the Qur'an in reward, as reported in Sahih al-Bukhari 5015.

Tawhid has practical weight. It means worship, du'a, hope, fear, reliance, and sacrifice are directed to Allah alone. Nothing created, no saint, no grave, no object, and no intermediary, has any right to any of that. Its opposite is shirk, associating partners with Allah, which is the one sin Allah has said He will not forgive if a person dies upon it (Qur'an 4:48). Getting tawhid right is the difference between a life accepted by Allah and one wasted.

The Five Pillars of Islam

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said Islam is built on five things, as narrated in Sahih al-Bukhari 8 and Sahih Muslim 16. These are the framework of a Muslim's practice.

  1. The Shahada. Testifying that there is no god worthy of worship except Allah, and that Muhammad is His Messenger. This is the entry point to Islam and the statement a person says and believes to become Muslim.
  2. Salah. The five daily prayers, performed at their set times. Prayer is the direct, daily connection between a servant and his Lord and the first deed a person will be asked about on the Day of Judgement.
  3. Zakah. An obligatory portion of wealth, given each year to those entitled to it, purifying the wealth and the one who gives it.
  4. Sawm. Fasting the month of Ramadan from dawn to sunset, abstaining from food, drink, and desire, as a training in patience and God-consciousness.
  5. Hajj. The pilgrimage to the Sacred House in Makkah, obligatory once in a lifetime upon whoever is able.

These five are acts of the limbs. On their own they are a body without a soul. The soul is belief.

The Six Pillars of Iman

Islam is not only outward action. It begins in the heart with iman, faith. When the angel Jibril came to question the Prophet (peace be upon him) about the religion, the Prophet listed the pillars of faith, as recorded in Sahih Muslim 8. A Muslim believes in:

  • Allah, in His oneness, His names, and His attributes.
  • His angels, created from light, who carry out His commands.
  • His Books, including the Torah, the Injil, and the final revelation, the Qur'an.
  • His Messengers, from the first to Muhammad, the last of them.
  • The Last Day, resurrection, the reckoning, Paradise, and the Fire.
  • Al-Qadr, divine decree, that what reached you could never have missed you, and what missed you could never have reached you.

This same hadith of Jibril mentions a third level: ihsan, excellence. The Prophet described it as worshipping Allah as though you see Him, and though you do not see Him, knowing that He sees you. Islam at its best is lived with that awareness.

The Qur'an and the Sunnah

Islam has two sources of guidance, and the religion is understood only through them.

The Qur'an is the literal speech of Allah, revealed to Muhammad (peace be upon him) over twenty-three years through the angel Jibril. It has been preserved word for word, exactly as Allah promised when He said that He sent down the Reminder and He will guard it (Qur'an 15:9). No other scripture on earth has that preservation.

The Sunnah is the authentic teaching, practice, and approval of the Prophet (peace be upon him). Allah commanded obedience to the Messenger alongside obedience to Himself (Qur'an 4:80), so the Sunnah is not optional commentary. It explains the Qur'an and shows us how to live it.

The safest way to understand both is the way of the Salaf as-Salih, the first three generations of Muslims whom the Prophet praised as the best of people in Sahih al-Bukhari 2652. They received the religion directly and passed it on without distortion. Clinging to their understanding keeps a Muslim away from later sectarianism and innovation.

Who Was Muhammad (peace be upon him)

Muhammad ibn Abdullah was born in Makkah and sent as the final Messenger to all of mankind. He did not come with a new god or a new message in essence, but to complete and seal the long line of prophethood. Allah describes him as a mercy to the worlds (Qur'an 21:107).

To follow Islam is to follow him. Allah tied the love of Himself to obedience of the Prophet, saying that if you truly love Allah, then follow the Messenger and Allah will love you (Qur'an 3:31). His life, honest in trade, truthful in speech, gentle with family, and firm in worship, is the living model of what Islam looks like in a human being.

How Islam Shapes a Life

Islam is not a weekend identity. It governs how a person earns, speaks, eats, treats his parents, raises children, and chooses a spouse. The Prophet (peace be upon him) said the most complete believers in faith are the best of them in character, as reported in Sunan al-Tirmidhi 1162. Belief and behaviour are meant to be one thing.

Marriage is a clear example. Islam dignifies it as half of the religion and a path to tranquillity, not a casual arrangement. Allah describes spouses as a source of peace and placed love and mercy between them (Qur'an 30:21). The Prophet advised choosing a spouse for their religion above all else, as in Sahih al-Bukhari 5090. That is why a marriage in Islam is built on deen and conducted properly, with a woman's walī, a mahr, and witnesses, rather than on dating or private entanglements that the Shariah does not sanction.

How Do You Become a Muslim?

Entering Islam is simple and requires no ceremony or clergy. A person declares the shahada with sincerity and conviction: Ash-hadu an la ilaha illa Allah, wa ash-hadu anna Muhammadan rasul Allah. This means, I bear witness that there is no god worthy of worship except Allah, and I bear witness that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah.

Saying it with understanding and belief makes a person Muslim, and Islam wipes away whatever sins came before it, as the Prophet taught in Sahih Muslim 121. From there, a new Muslim learns the prayer, the basics of tawhid, and grows step by step.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does the word Islam actually mean?

It means submission and surrender to Allah alone, with the inner peace that follows from it. A Muslim is literally one who submits to his Lord.

Is Islam a completely new religion?

No. It is the final form of the one message every prophet brought, from Adam to Muhammad: worship Allah alone. Allah calls it the only accepted religion with Him in Qur'an 3:19.

What is the difference between Islam and iman?

In the hadith of Jibril (Sahih Muslim 8), Islam refers to the outward acts of worship like the five pillars, while iman is the inward belief, such as belief in Allah, the angels, and the Last Day. A complete Muslim has both.

What are the holy books of Islam?

Muslims believe Allah revealed scriptures to earlier prophets, including the Torah to Musa and the Injil to Isa. The final and preserved revelation is the Qur'an, which Allah promised to protect in Qur'an 15:9.

Why is tawhid so central?

Because it is the entire point of creation and the one matter on which salvation depends. Allah forgives other sins for whom He wills, but not dying upon shirk (Qur'an 4:48). Everything else in Islam is built on getting this right.

How does a person become a Muslim?

By sincerely declaring the shahada: that there is no god worthy of worship except Allah, and that Muhammad is His Messenger. It needs no clergy or ritual, and it erases the sins that came before it.
