The Walima: The Marriage Feast in the Sunnah

﷽

In the name of Allah, the Most Merciful, the Bestower of Mercy.

We try to follow and explain from the Qur'an and the authentic Hadith, upon the understanding of the three rightly-guided generations (the Salaf as-Salih). All good is from Allah alone, and any mistake or shortcoming is from ourselves.

TL;DR: The walima is the marriage feast the husband hosts to announce and celebrate his nikah. It is a confirmed Sunnah (sunnah mu'akkadah), not a fard, and the Sunnah is that it be simple and sincere, not extravagant. Even one sheep suffices, and even less. Those invited are obligated to attend unless they have a valid excuse.

What the Walima Actually Is

The walima is the meal a man provides after his marriage to announce it publicly and to express gratitude to Allah for the blessing of a spouse. The word itself carries the sense of gathering, because a marriage brings two people and two families together.

Marriage is a matter Allah dignified in His Book. He said that He created spouses "that you may find tranquility in them, and He placed between you affection and mercy" (Qur'an 30:21). The walima is the outward, communal side of that private bond. It tells the community that this union happened openly, with witnesses and celebration, and it draws a clear line between a lawful marriage and anything hidden or shameful. Announcing the nikah is part of the deen, and the feast is one of the ways that announcement is made.

If you are still working through the mechanics of the marriage itself, the walima sits at the end of that process, after the nikah contract is concluded. It is the celebration that follows the agreement, not the agreement itself.

Is the Walima Fard or Sunnah?

The walima is a confirmed Sunnah of the Prophet, upon him be peace. He did it himself and he instructed the companions to do it. The stronger position among the scholars is that it is Sunnah mu'akkadah, a stressed and recommended practice, rather than an outright obligation. It is not something a marriage is invalid without, but it is something a Muslim should not casually abandon.

The clearest proof is the narration of Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf. When the Prophet, upon him be peace, saw the trace of marriage on him, he told him: "Give a walima, even with one sheep" (Sahih al-Bukhari 5167). The instruction is real, but the wording shows how low the bar sits. One sheep. And the scholars note that even this is by way of encouragement, not a threat of sin for the one who cannot manage it.

The Prophet, upon him be peace, held a walima for his own marriages. For his marriage to Zaynab he fed the people bread and meat (Sahih al-Bukhari 5171). On another occasion his walima was far more modest, provided from two mudds of barley. The point is not the size of the feast. The point is that the marriage was announced and Allah was thanked.

When Is the Walima Held?

The walima is tied to the marriage, and the practice of the Prophet, upon him be peace, was to hold it around the time the couple came together, after the consummation on the strongest reading of the reports about his own walimas. Some hold it can be at the time of the contract, some after. This is a matter of preference and custom, not a fixed ruling, so there is breadth here.

What matters is that there is genuinely a walima, that it is connected to the marriage, and that it fulfils the purpose of announcing the union. Do not let arguments over the exact day become a source of family friction. Choose a time that lets you gather people and feed them, and keep the intention on thanking Allah rather than on performing for guests.

Keep It Simple: This Is the Sunnah, Not a Concession

The defining feature of a Sunnah walima is simplicity. This is worth stressing, because culture pushes hard in the opposite direction. Families sink themselves into debt, borrow heavily, and treat the feast as a competition. None of that is from the deen. It is often the opposite of the deen.

Allah is direct about waste. He said, "Eat and drink, but be not excessive. Indeed, He does not like those who commit excess" (Qur'an 7:31). And He said of the wasteful that "the wasteful are brothers of the devils" (Qur'an 17:27). A feast that ruins the family financially, or that piles up food to be thrown away for the sake of appearances, has traded the blessing of the Sunnah for something Allah dislikes.

The Prophet's own walimas ranged from meat and bread down to a simple dish of barley. A man should feed people according to his means. If he can afford a sheep, good. If he can afford dates and something to drink, that is also a walima. Debt and display are not requirements. Sincerity and generosity within your means are. A marriage that begins with the couple free of debt and free of the anxiety of a lavish event is a marriage that begins on firmer ground. Do Not Invite Only the Wealthy

There is a specific warning in the Sunnah about who gets invited. The Prophet, upon him be peace, said: "The worst food is the food of a walima to which the rich are invited and the poor are left out" (Sahih al-Bukhari 5177, and in Sahih Muslim 1432).

This cuts against the entire logic of a status feast. A walima is not a networking event or a showcase for the well-connected. It is a gathering where the poor should be fed alongside the rich, where relatives and neighbours are welcomed regardless of what they can give back. When you write the guest list, remember that the food of a walima is judged, in the words of the Prophet, upon him be peace, by whether it reached those who needed it or only those who impress.

Accepting the Invitation Is an Obligation

The walima carries a duty not only for the host but for the guest. The Prophet, upon him be peace, said: "When one of you is invited to a walima, let him attend" (Sahih al-Bukhari 5173, and in Sahih Muslim 1429). In the same context he said, regarding the one who refuses the invitation without excuse, that he has disobeyed Allah and His Messenger (Sahih al-Bukhari 5177).

So answering a walima invitation is not optional courtesy. It is a right the host has upon you. The scholars mention valid excuses that lift this duty: if the gathering contains open disobedience such as alcohol or unlawful entertainment and you cannot remove it, if there is genuine harm to attending, or a real conflicting need. If you are fasting a voluntary fast, still attend. The Prophet, upon him be peace, taught that the one who is fasting should attend and make du'a for the hosts (Sahih Muslim 1431). You honour the invitation with your presence even if you do not eat.

Keeping the Walima Within the Bounds of the Shariah

A walima is a celebration, but it is still a Muslim gathering, and it stays inside the same limits as the rest of your life. A few things need to be settled before the day, not argued about on it.

Free-mixing is the most common problem. A walima where men and women are pushed together in one space, with all the attendant loosening of behaviour, has invited fitnah into what should be a blessed occasion. The straightforward solution, and the one that spares everyone, is to keep the gathering arranged so that men and women are not mixing freely. Many families do this simply by separating the space. This is not harshness. It is protecting the very thing you are celebrating.

Unlawful entertainment is the second. A wedding is a place where lawful joy and open announcement are encouraged, and the duff and permissible songs of celebration have a basis in the Sunnah for this occasion. What is not from the Sunnah is instrumental music, indecency, and the trappings of the surrounding culture that no serious Muslim would defend on their own. Keep the celebration clean and it remains a celebration Allah is thanked in, not one He is disobeyed in.

The third is intention. The whole point of the walima is gratitude to Allah and open announcement of a lawful bond. Hold onto that, and the decisions about budget, guest list, and format tend to sort themselves out. If your marriage began the right way, with clear intention and a proper process rather than the modern drift of dating and talking stages, then let the walima carry the same spirit. Frequently Asked Questions

Is the walima obligatory?

The stronger view is that it is a confirmed Sunnah (sunnah mu'akkadah), strongly recommended rather than fard. The Prophet, upon him be peace, commanded Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf to hold one "even with one sheep" (Sahih al-Bukhari 5167), which shows both its importance and how modest it may be. A man should not neglect it, but a marriage is not invalid without it.

Who pays for the walima?

By the Sunnah, the walima is hosted by the husband. It is his feast for his marriage. Families often help, and there is no harm in that, but the responsibility and the honour of it belong to the groom.

How big does the walima have to be?

As big as your means allow and no bigger. The Prophet's walimas ranged from meat and bread down to a simple barley dish. Do not borrow or go into debt to impress people. A modest, sincere meal that includes the poor is closer to the Sunnah than a lavish one that leaves them out.

Can the walima have music and mixed seating?

Instrumental music and free-mixing should be avoided. The duff and clean songs of celebration have a basis for weddings, but the wider culture of unlawful entertainment does not. Arrange the gathering so men and women are not mixing freely, and keep the celebration within the limits of the Shariah.

Do I have to accept a walima invitation?

Yes, unless you have a valid excuse. The Prophet, upon him be peace, said to attend when invited (Sahih al-Bukhari 5173). If the gathering contains open sin you cannot prevent, that is a recognised excuse. If you are fasting, still attend and make du'a for the hosts.

When should the walima be held?

It is tied to the marriage and is customarily held around the time the couple comes together. There is breadth in the timing, and it is not a fixed ruling, so choose what suits your family without turning it into a dispute.
