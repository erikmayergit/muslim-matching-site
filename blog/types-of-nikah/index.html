Types of Nikah: What Islam Accepts and What It Forbids

﷽

In the name of Allah, the Most Merciful, the Bestower of Mercy.

We try to follow and explain from the Qur'an and the authentic Hadith, upon the understanding of the three rightly-guided generations (the Salaf as-Salih). All good is from Allah alone, and any mistake or shortcoming is from ourselves.

TL;DR: In Islam there is really only one valid marriage, the standard permanent nikah built on the woman's consent, her walī, the mahr, and witnesses. Beyond that, the labels people throw around (mutʿah, shighār, tahlīl, so-called secret or online marriages) are mostly names for contracts that are either forbidden outright or invalid. This guide sorts the recognised types of nikah from the corrupted ones, with the evidence.

The one nikah Islam actually recognises

Most of the confusion around the "types of nikah" disappears once you understand a simple point. Islam legislated one marriage. Everything else is a variation people invented, and each variation is judged by whether it keeps or breaks the conditions Allah set.

Allah describes marriage as a source of tranquillity and mercy: "And among His signs is that He created for you from yourselves mates that you may find tranquillity in them, and He placed between you affection and mercy" (Qur'an 30:21). He commands the community to marry off its single members: "And marry the unmarried among you and the righteous among your male and female slaves" (Qur'an 24:32).

A valid nikah stands on a handful of pillars and conditions. Get these right and the marriage is sound. Miss one of the essentials and you no longer have a nikah, whatever you choose to call it.

The conditions of a sound marriage

  • The consent of both parties. A woman cannot be forced. The Prophet (peace be upon him) said a previously married woman must not be married until she is consulted, and a virgin must not be married until her permission is sought (Sahih al-Bukhari 5136).
  • The walī (guardian). A woman's marriage is contracted through her guardian. The Prophet (peace be upon him) said, "There is no marriage without a walī" (Sunan Abi Dawud 2085, authentic). This is not a cultural formality. It is a condition of validity, and no woman marries herself off. We cover it in depth in the role of the wali in marriage.
  • The mahr. A gift given by the husband to the wife as her right: "And give the women their dowries graciously" (Qur'an 4:4). It can be modest, but it must exist.
  • Witnesses and openness. The marriage must be witnessed and made known, not hidden away. This is what separates nikah from a secret arrangement.

When all of this is present, the marriage is simply nikah. There is no need for adjectives. The various named "types" below are best understood as departures from this standard, some tolerated, most rejected. If you want the full picture of the process, see how to get married in Islam and the nikah contract.

Nikah al-Mutʿah: temporary marriage (forbidden)

Mutʿah is a contract in which a man and woman agree to marry for a fixed period, after which the marriage automatically ends. It was permitted at a very early point in Islam and then prohibited by the Prophet (peace be upon him) himself.

ʿAli ibn Abi Talib reported that the Messenger of Allah (peace be upon him) forbade the temporary marriage of women and the eating of the meat of domestic donkeys at the time of Khaybar (Sahih al-Bukhari 5115). In Sahih Muslim it is recorded that the Prophet (peace be upon him) prohibited mutʿah, and in one narration the prohibition was declared to stand "until the Day of Resurrection" (Sahih Muslim 1406).

The scholars of the Sunnah are agreed that mutʿah is forbidden. A marriage entered with a built-in expiry, or a hidden intention to divorce after a set time, is not the tranquil, lasting bond Allah described. It reduces the wife to a temporary convenience and strips away the responsibilities marriage is meant to carry. Do not be misled by anyone who revives it under a new name.

Nikah al-Shighar: exchange marriage (forbidden)

Shighar is when two men marry off their daughters or sisters to each other on the understanding that one woman is the "payment" for the other, with no real mahr changing hands. In effect the women are traded.

The Prophet (peace be upon him) forbade shighar (Sahih al-Bukhari 5112, Sahih Muslim 1415). The narration explains it as a man giving his daughter in marriage on the condition that the other gives him his daughter, with no dowry between them.

The problem is twofold. It cancels the woman's right to her own mahr, and it makes her a bargaining chip in someone else's deal. Both violate her rights. A marriage where a woman is used as currency for another marriage is corrupt from the outset.

Nikah al-Tahlīl (Halala): the "lawful-maker" marriage (forbidden and cursed)

When a man divorces his wife the third and final time, she becomes forbidden to him until she marries another man in a genuine marriage, and that second marriage ends naturally through his death or a real divorce (Qur'an 2:230).

Tahlīl, often called halala, corrupts this. A man is arranged to marry the woman for the sole purpose of divorcing her afterward so she can return to her first husband. It is a sham contract with a hidden expiry, dressed up to look legitimate.

The Prophet (peace be upon him) cursed the muhallil (the man who marries to make her lawful again) and the muhallal lahu (the first husband for whom it is done), as reported in Jamiʿ at-Tirmidhi 1120. A contract that carries the curse of the Messenger of Allah is not a valid type of nikah. It is a sin arranged to look like worship. The genuine second marriage the verse speaks of is a real marriage entered with no plan to divorce, not a staged transaction.

Secret marriage: no witnesses, no openness (invalid)

Some try to contract a nikah in complete secrecy, sometimes to hide it from parents, sometimes to escape the walī requirement altogether. This is not a recognised type of marriage. It is a corruption of it.

Marriage in Islam is meant to be public and witnessed. It is the opposite of a hidden relationship. Once you remove the walī and the witnesses, what remains is not a quieter version of nikah. It fails the conditions of validity. A woman who arranges her own "marriage" without her guardian has, per the hadith of the walī above, no valid marriage at all. Do not let anyone convince you that a private ceremony with a couple of friends, hidden from her family, is a Shariah nikah. It is closer to the very thing marriage exists to prevent.

Misyar and other modern arrangements (contested, tread carefully)

Misyar is a modern arrangement, common in some regions, in which all the pillars of nikah are present (walī, consent, mahr, witnesses) but the wife voluntarily waives some of her rights, such as the right to housing or to equal nights if the husband has another wife.

This one is genuinely contested among contemporary scholars. Some permit it on the basis that all the conditions of a valid nikah are met and a spouse may choose to forgo certain personal rights. Others discourage or reject it, warning that in practice it often mimics mutʿah, isolates the woman, produces neglected children, and is used to sidestep responsibility.

Because this is a live difference of opinion and not a settled matter, we will not hand down a verdict here. If you are considering it, do not rely on a blog post. Put your exact situation to a trustworthy scholar grounded in the Qur'an and Sunnah upon the understanding of the Salaf, and act on their guidance.

Polygyny: more than one wife within the limits (permitted)

Marrying more than one wife, up to four, is not a separate "type" of nikah so much as the standard nikah repeated within the limits Allah set: "then marry those that please you of women, two or three or four. But if you fear that you will not be just, then only one" (Qur'an 4:3).

Each marriage must independently meet every condition, a walī for each wife, a mahr for each, witnesses for each, and the husband is bound to deal justly between them. This is permitted in the Shariah and should not be spoken of with the embarrassment some import from other cultures. But it is a responsibility before it is a right, and injustice between wives is a serious matter.

What all of this means for you

Strip away the labels and the picture is simple. There is one nikah. It is the marriage that keeps the consent of the woman, the walī, the mahr, and open witnessing intact. The forbidden "types," mutʿah, shighār, and tahlīl, all share one trait: they smuggle in a hidden intention or cancel a right that Allah made non-negotiable. The invalid ones, like the secret self-marriage, simply drop a condition and hope no one notices.

So the practical advice is not to go hunting for a special category. It is to do the ordinary nikah properly, with the right intention from the start. Approach marriage seriously, involve the walī, treat the mahr as a real right, and keep everything above board. That is what a platform built for this should help you do. Frequently Asked Questions

How many valid types of nikah are there in Islam?

Essentially one: the standard permanent marriage that fulfils consent, the walī, the mahr, and witnesses. Polygyny is that same marriage contracted more than once within the limit of four, with justice between wives. The other "types" people list are either forbidden or invalid.

Is temporary marriage (mutʿah) allowed?

No. It was permitted early in Islam and then prohibited by the Prophet (peace be upon him), as in Sahih al-Bukhari 5115 and Sahih Muslim 1406. The scholars of the Sunnah agree it is forbidden. Any marriage with a hidden or built-in expiry falls under this prohibition.

Is a marriage valid without the walī?

No. The Prophet (peace be upon him) said, "There is no marriage without a walī" (Sunan Abi Dawud 2085). A woman does not contract her own marriage. The guardian is a condition of validity, not an optional custom, and anyone telling you to bypass it is steering you toward an invalid contract.

What is halala and why is it forbidden?

Halala (tahlīl) is arranging a marriage whose only purpose is to divorce afterward so a woman can remarry her first husband after a triple divorce. The Prophet (peace be upon him) cursed both the man who does this and the one it is done for (Jamiʿ at-Tirmidhi 1120). Only a genuine, sincere marriage counts, never a staged one.

Is misyar marriage halal?

It is contested. It keeps the pillars of nikah but involves the wife waiving certain rights. Some scholars permit it, others reject it because in practice it can resemble mutʿah and lead to neglect. This is not something to settle from an article. Ask a qualified scholar about your specific case.

Does the marriage have to be public?

Yes, it must be witnessed and openly known, not hidden. Concealed marriages arranged to escape the family or the walī are not a valid category of nikah. Openness is part of what distinguishes marriage from a forbidden secret relationship.
