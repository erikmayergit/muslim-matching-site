Sunnah Nikah: How the Prophet ﷺ Taught Us to Marry

﷽

In the name of Allah, the Most Merciful, the Bestower of Mercy.

We try to follow and explain from the Qur'an and the authentic Hadith, upon the understanding of the three rightly-guided generations (the Salaf as-Salih). All good is from Allah alone, and any mistake or shortcoming is from ourselves.

TL;DR: A Sunnah nikah is a marriage kept simple and lawful: a woman is given by her walī, two upright witnesses are present, a mahr is agreed, and offer and acceptance are spoken. The Prophet ﷺ praised the marriage that is easiest in burden, so the Sunnah way strips out extravagance and cultural excess and centres deen, character, barakah, and a public announcement.

What "Sunnah Nikah" Actually Means

A Sunnah nikah is not a special ceremony with a fixed script. It is marriage conducted the way the Prophet ﷺ and his Companions conducted it: valid in its pillars, light in its burden, honest in its intention, and free of the customs people later attached to it and mistook for religion.

Allah calls marriage a sign of His mercy: "And of His signs is that He created for you from yourselves mates that you may find tranquillity in them, and He placed between you affection and mercy" (Qur'an 30:21). The aim is tranquillity and mercy, not display. Once you understand that, most of what makes a nikah stressful and expensive falls away, because none of it was ever required.

The deen encourages marriage strongly. Allah commands, "And marry the unmarried among you" (Qur'an 24:32), and the Prophet ﷺ said that whoever is able should marry, for it lowers the gaze and guards chastity (Sahih al-Bukhari 5065). A Sunnah nikah is simply how you answer that encouragement without over-complicating it.

The Pillars: What Makes a Nikah Valid

Before any custom or celebration, a marriage stands or falls on its conditions. Get these right and you have a valid nikah in the sight of Allah. Miss one and no amount of ceremony fixes it. For a fuller treatment, see our guide to the nikah contract.

The Walī (Guardian)

A woman does not marry herself off. Her marriage is contracted by her walī, normally her father, then the nearest male relatives in order. The Prophet ﷺ said, "There is no marriage without a walī" (Sunan Abi Dawud 2085, authentic), and he said, "Any woman who marries without the permission of her walī, her marriage is invalid" (Sunan Abi Dawud 2083, authentic). This is a firm requirement, not a formality to be worked around. The walī protects the woman's interest and represents her family; he does not force her, since her consent is also required.

Two Upright Witnesses

A nikah is a public matter, not a secret arrangement between two people. The presence of witnesses distinguishes a lawful marriage from a hidden relationship. This, together with the walī, is what removes any ambiguity about the union and protects the rights of both spouses and any children.

The Mahr

The mahr is the gift the husband gives the wife, and it is her right alone. "And give the women their dowries graciously" (Qur'an 4:4). It has no fixed minimum in a way that should burden a man. The Prophet ﷺ told a man who had nothing to teach his wife the portions of the Qur'an he knew as her mahr (Sahih al-Bukhari 5087), and he told another to give a mahr even if only an iron ring. The Sunnah is that the mahr is real and honoured, but reasonable. We cover this in detail in the guide to the mahr.

Offer and Acceptance

The contract itself is the spoken offer (from the walī) and acceptance (from the groom) in the presence of the witnesses. This is the moment the two become husband and wife. It is short, clear, and needs no elaborate wording.

Choosing a Spouse the Sunnah Way

A Sunnah nikah begins long before the contract, in how you choose. The Prophet ﷺ said a woman is married for four things: her wealth, her lineage, her beauty, and her religion, then instructed, "so choose the one of religion, may your hands be rubbed with dust" (Sahih al-Bukhari 5090). He gave the same standard to guardians: "If there comes to you one whose religion and character please you, then marry him" (Sunan al-Tirmidhi 1084, hasan).

So the two filters that matter most are deen and character. Everything else is secondary. Keeping It Simple: The Sunnah of Ease

This is where most people drift furthest from the Sunnah. The Prophet ﷺ praised the marriage of least burden and ease. The best mahr is the one that is easy. He married and gave his own daughters in marriage without lavish spending, and the Companions followed that. When 'Abd al-Rahman ibn 'Awf married, the Prophet ﷺ did not ask about a grand event; he simply told him to hold a walima (Sahih al-Bukhari 5155).

The practical meaning is clear. Do not sink families into debt. Do not make the mahr a bidding war. Do not delay a good marriage for years to save for a hall and a guest list. Ease removes the excuses that push young Muslims toward the haram while they wait for a wedding they cannot afford. A Sunnah nikah says: marry now, marry within your means, and let barakah do the rest.

The Walima

The walima is the marriage feast the husband gives after consummation, and it is a confirmed Sunnah. The Prophet ﷺ told 'Abd al-Rahman ibn 'Awf, "Give a walima, even with one sheep" (Sahih al-Bukhari 5167). Notice the scale: even a single sheep suffices. The walima is meant to be a genuine act of gratitude and a sharing of food, not a competition of expense.

Accepting an invitation to a walima is emphasised in the Sunnah, and the Prophet ﷺ criticised the food of a walima to which the rich are invited and the poor are shunned (Sahih al-Bukhari 5177). So the Sunnah walima is inclusive and modest, feeding people who might not otherwise be fed, not a display for those who already have everything.

Announcing the Marriage

Marriage is meant to be known, not hidden. The Prophet ﷺ said, "Announce this marriage" (Sunan al-Tirmidhi 1089). Part of the permitted celebration is the beating of the duff (a simple frame drum) by the women and lawful singing free of anything indecent, as the Prophet ﷺ mentioned that what distinguishes the lawful from the unlawful in marriage is the duff and the voice (Sunan al-Tirmidhi 1088, hasan).

This is the extent of what is established: a public announcement, the duff, and modest joy within the bounds of the Shariah. It is not a licence for free-mixing, music built on unlawful instruments, or immodesty. Announce the marriage, keep the gathering clean, and the celebration itself becomes an act rewarded rather than a night of regret.

Du'as and the Wedding Night

The Sunnah gives the couple words to say from the very beginning. When someone marries, the Companions would greet him with the du'a the Prophet ﷺ taught: "Bārak Allāhu laka, wa bāraka 'alayka, wa jama'a baynakumā fī khayr" (May Allah bless you, and send blessings upon you, and join you together in goodness) (Sunan al-Tirmidhi 1091, authentic).

On the wedding night, before intimacy, the Prophet ﷺ taught the du'a: "Bismillāh, Allāhumma jannibnā ash-shaytān wa jannib ash-shaytāna mā razaqtanā" (In the name of Allah. O Allah, keep the devil away from us and keep the devil away from what You provide us) (Sahih al-Bukhari 5165). The Prophet ﷺ said that if a child is decreed for them, the devil will never harm it. It is also reported from the Companions that the husband place his hand on his wife's forehead and pray for the good in her and ask refuge from her ill, and it is reported from the practice of the Salaf that the couple pray two rak'ah together before their first night. Begin the marriage with the remembrance of Allah, and you begin it well.

Cultural Add-ons Mistaken for Religion

Much of what stresses families at a wedding has no basis in the deen. Extravagant, debt-inducing mahr demands; days of obligatory rituals borrowed from local custom; segregation ignored and free-mixing normalised; music and displays that would embarrass a believer; wasteful spending Allah warns against when He says the wasteful are the brothers of the devils (Qur'an 17:27). None of this is the Sunnah, and presenting a cultural custom as if it were the religion is itself a serious matter.

Strip the nikah back to what Allah and His Messenger ﷺ actually asked for, and you are left with something light, dignified, and full of barakah. That is the whole point of doing it the Sunnah way. When you are ready to begin on that foundation, you can join the Muslim Matching waitlist.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the essential sunnah practices of a nikah? The core is a valid contract (walī, two witnesses, mahr, offer and acceptance) built on choosing for deen and character. The recommended practices around it include keeping the mahr and costs easy, announcing the marriage, the duff and modest celebration, holding a walima afterward, and reciting the taught du'as for the couple and for the wedding night.

Is a walī really required, or can a woman marry herself off? Required. The Prophet ﷺ said there is no marriage without a walī and that a marriage contracted without one is invalid (Sunan Abi Dawud 2085). The woman's consent is also required, so it is neither self-marriage nor forced marriage. The walī contracts, the bride agrees.

How much should the mahr be for a sunnah nikah? There is no fixed amount, and the Sunnah leans toward ease. The Prophet ﷺ accepted a mahr as small as an iron ring or teaching Qur'an (Sahih al-Bukhari 5087). It must be something real and agreed upon, given to the wife as her right, but it should not be inflated to burden the groom.

Is the walima obligatory? It is a strongly confirmed Sunnah, not a pillar of the contract. The Prophet ﷺ instructed 'Abd al-Rahman ibn 'Awf to hold one "even with one sheep" (Sahih al-Bukhari 5167). It should be modest and inclusive of the poor, and accepting an invitation to it is emphasised.

Can we have music and celebration at a sunnah nikah? The established Sunnah is announcing the marriage with the duff (a simple frame drum) and lawful, decent singing (Sunan al-Tirmidhi 1088). This does not extend to unlawful instruments, indecent lyrics, free-mixing, or immodesty. Keep the joy within the limits of the Shariah.

What du'a is made on the wedding night? Before intimacy the couple says, "Bismillāh, Allāhumma jannibnā ash-shaytān wa jannib ash-shaytāna mā razaqtanā" (Sahih al-Bukhari 5165). The husband is also greeted with the du'a for barakah and being joined in goodness (Sunan al-Tirmidhi 1091).
