Ramadan 2026: When It Starts and How to Track the Days

﷽

In the name of Allah, the Most Merciful, the Bestower of Mercy.

We try to follow and explain from the Qur'an and the authentic Hadith, upon the understanding of the three rightly-guided generations (the Salaf as-Salih). All good is from Allah alone, and any mistake or shortcoming is from ourselves.

TL;DR: Ramadan 2026 (1447 AH) is expected to begin at sunset on Tuesday, 17 February 2026, with the first day of fasting on Wednesday, 18 February 2026. This is an astronomical estimate. The real start depends on the sighting of the crescent moon (ru'yah), so your local date may shift by a day.

When Does Ramadan 2026 Begin?

Based on astronomical calculation, the new moon of Ramadan 1447 AH falls in mid-February 2026. The widely expected timeline is:

  • First taraweeh: night of Tuesday, 17 February 2026
  • First fast (1 Ramadan): Wednesday, 18 February 2026

Treat these as estimates, not a verdict. Calculation tells us when the moon is born and roughly when it can be seen. It does not, by itself, decide when the month begins for us. That is settled by sighting the crescent, and different regions confirm on slightly different evenings. So the honest answer to "ramadan time 2026" is: around 18 February, confirmed by your local moon-sighting authority a day or two before.

Why the Date Is Not Fixed in Advance

The Islamic month begins with the actual sighting of the new crescent, not with a printed calendar. This is the clear instruction of the Prophet (sallallahu alayhi wa sallam), who said: "Fast when you see it (the crescent) and stop fasting when you see it, and if it is obscured from you, then complete thirty days of Sha'ban." This is reported in Sahih al-Bukhari 1909 and Sahih Muslim 1081.

Allah tied the fast to the arrival of the month itself: "So whoever among you sights the month, let him fast it" (Qur'an 2:185).

Because the crescent may be seen on one evening in some lands and only the next evening elsewhere, a single fixed global date cannot be guaranteed years ahead. This is why serious calendars mark February 2026 as expected and defer to the sighting. Rely on a trustworthy local sighting committee or masjid rather than on calculation alone.

Ramadan 2026 Countdown and Which-Day Tracker

Use this as a simple day-by-day guide once the month is confirmed. If the first fast is Wednesday, 18 February 2026, the key markers fall as follows (each Islamic day begins at the preceding sunset):

  • 1 Ramadan: Wednesday, 18 February 2026
  • 10 Ramadan: Friday, 27 February 2026
  • 20 Ramadan (last ten nights begin the night of the 20th): Monday, 9 March 2026
  • 21, 23, 25, 27, 29 Ramadan (odd nights of the last ten): the strongest candidates for Laylatul Qadr
  • Expected Eid al-Fitr (1 Shawwal): around Thursday or Friday, 19 to 20 March 2026, depending on whether Ramadan is 29 or 30 days

To know which day it is at a glance during the month, count from the confirmed first fast. If today is 25 February and the first fast was 18 February, you are on the 8th day. Keep the count tied to sunset, since the night comes before the day in the Islamic reckoning.

What Ramadan Is Actually For

Ramadan is not merely a schedule of hunger and late nights. Allah made its purpose explicit: "O you who believe, fasting is prescribed for you as it was prescribed for those before you, that you may attain taqwa" (Qur'an 2:183). The month is a training ground for God-consciousness, restraint, and worship.

It is also the month of the Qur'an: "The month of Ramadan in which the Qur'an was revealed, a guidance for mankind" (Qur'an 2:185). The reward is immense for the one who approaches it sincerely. The Prophet (sallallahu alayhi wa sallam) said: "Whoever fasts Ramadan out of faith and seeking reward, his previous sins will be forgiven," reported in Sahih al-Bukhari 38.

So the countdown is not the point. The preparation of the heart is.

The Last Ten Nights and Laylatul Qadr

The final ten nights carry the heaviest weight of the month. Allah describes Laylatul Qadr as "better than a thousand months" (Qur'an 97:3). The Prophet (sallallahu alayhi wa sallam) instructed us to pursue it: "Search for Laylatul Qadr in the odd nights of the last ten of Ramadan," reported in Sahih al-Bukhari 2017.

In 2026, if the month begins on 18 February, the odd nights of the last ten fall on the eves of the 21st, 23rd, 25th, 27th, and 29th of Ramadan, roughly the second week of March. Do not gamble on a single night. The Prophet (sallallahu alayhi wa sallam) would strive in worship across all ten, tightening his effort and waking his family. Plan your leave, your qiyam, and your Qur'an around this window rather than treating the whole month at one flat pace.

How to Prepare Before Ramadan 2026 Arrives

The weeks of Sha'ban before Ramadan are the runway. A few concrete steps:

  • Settle any missed fasts from the previous Ramadan before the new one begins, if you still owe them.
  • Build the habit early. The Prophet (sallallahu alayhi wa sallam) fasted frequently in Sha'ban, as reported by his wife Aisha in Sahih al-Bukhari 1969. Voluntary fasts now make the obligatory fasts easier later.
  • Fix your relationship with the Qur'an. Set a daily portion you can actually keep.
  • Clear your calendar of what will pull you away from worship in the last ten nights.

Going in with a plan is the difference between a month that transforms you and a month that simply passes.

Ramadan, Intention, and Marriage

Ramadan sharpens intention, and intention is exactly where a serious search for a spouse should begin. Many Muslims use this month to make sincere du'a for a righteous partner and to resolve, honestly, to pursue marriage the halal way rather than drifting.

Marriage in Islam is itself an act of worship and a means of tranquillity: "And among His signs is that He created for you mates from among yourselves, that you may find tranquillity in them, and He placed between you affection and mercy" (Qur'an 30:21). The Prophet (sallallahu alayhi wa sallam) also urged the young to marry and described half the deen as being completed by it, guidance recorded in Sahih al-Bukhari 5065.

And note how Allah frames the marriage bond in the very verses about Ramadan nights: spouses are described as garments for one another (Qur'an 2:187), a picture of closeness, protection, and dignity. If your du'a this Ramadan is for a spouse, let it be matched by action that keeps within the Shariah: no free-mixing, no private "talking stage," and the woman's marriage concluded through her walī. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the exact date of Ramadan 2026?

The expected first day of fasting is Wednesday, 18 February 2026, with taraweeh beginning the night before. This is an estimate based on calculation. The confirmed date depends on the crescent moon sighting and may move by a day in your region.

Why do calendars only say "expected" for Ramadan?

Because the month begins with the actual sighting of the crescent, as commanded in Sahih al-Bukhari 1909, not with astronomical calculation alone. Sighting can happen a day apart between regions, so no date years ahead is final.

When will Laylatul Qadr be in 2026?

It falls somewhere in the last ten nights, most likely on an odd night. If Ramadan starts on 18 February 2026, the odd nights are the eves of the 21st, 23rd, 25th, 27th, and 29th of Ramadan, around the second week of March. Worship across all ten rather than one.

When is Eid al-Fitr 2026?

Eid al-Fitr (1 Shawwal 1447) is expected around 19 to 20 March 2026, depending on whether Ramadan completes 29 or 30 days and on the sighting of the Shawwal crescent.

Should I follow calculation or moon sighting for the start of Ramadan?

The practice grounded in the Sunnah is to begin and end the month by sighting the crescent, completing thirty days of Sha'ban if it is not seen. Follow a reliable local sighting authority. Where scholars differ on details of regional versus global sighting, defer to trustworthy scholars rather than deciding on your own.

How should I prepare in the weeks before Ramadan?

Pay back any missed fasts, increase voluntary fasting in Sha'ban following the Prophet's example in Sahih al-Bukhari 1969, set a daily Qur'an portion, and plan your last ten nights in advance so the month is not wasted.
