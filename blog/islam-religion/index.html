Islam, the Religion of Submission to Allah: A Clear Guide

﷽

In the name of Allah, the Most Merciful, the Bestower of Mercy.

We try to follow and explain from the Qur'an and the authentic Hadith, upon the understanding of the three rightly-guided generations (the Salaf as-Salih). All good is from Allah alone, and any mistake or shortcoming is from ourselves.

TL;DR: Islam is the religion of submitting to Allah alone, worshipping Him without partners, and following the final Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) as preserved in the Qur'an and the authentic Sunnah. Its foundation is tawhid, its framework is the five pillars and the six articles of faith, and its purpose is to live in obedience to the One who created you.

What Islam Actually Is

The word Islam means submission and surrender to Allah with willing obedience. A Muslim is one who submits. This is not a culture, an ethnicity, or an inherited label. It is a relationship between the servant and his Creator, built on worshipping Him alone and accepting what He revealed.

Islam is not presented in the Qur'an as one option among many. It is presented as the religion Allah chose and completed. Allah says the meaning of, "This day I have perfected for you your religion and completed My favour upon you and have approved for you Islam as religion" (Qur'an 5:3). And He says, "Indeed, the religion in the sight of Allah is Islam" (Qur'an 3:19).

Islam also teaches that this is the same core message every prophet brought. Nuh, Ibrahim, Musa, Isa, and all the messengers called their people to worship Allah alone. Muhammad (peace be upon him) is the final link in that chain, and his message is for all of humanity until the Last Day.

Tawhid: The Heart of the Religion

Everything in Islam rests on tawhid, the oneness of Allah. To affirm tawhid is to believe that Allah alone is the Creator, that He alone deserves worship, and that His names and attributes are perfect and belong to Him alone without resemblance to the creation.

The opposite of tawhid is shirk, associating partners with Allah, and it is the one sin Allah does not forgive if a person dies upon it. Allah says the meaning of, "Indeed, Allah does not forgive association with Him, but He forgives what is less than that for whom He wills" (Qur'an 4:48).

This is why the entire religion begins with the testimony that there is no god worthy of worship except Allah. Worship, supplication, reliance, hope, fear, and sacrifice are directed to Him alone. No saint, grave, intermediary, or created being shares in that right.

The Two Sources: Qur'an and Authentic Sunnah

Islam is not built on guesswork or personal taste. It is built on two preserved sources: the Qur'an, the literal speech of Allah revealed to Muhammad (peace be upon him), and the authentic Sunnah, his sayings, actions, and approvals.

Allah commanded obedience to the Messenger alongside obedience to Himself: "Whoever obeys the Messenger has obeyed Allah" (Qur'an 4:80). The Prophet (peace be upon him) also warned against inventing new practices in the religion. He said, "Whoever introduces into this affair of ours something that is not from it will have it rejected" (Sahih al-Bukhari 2697).

To understand these two sources correctly, Muslims look to how the first three generations understood and practised them, the Salaf as-Salih: the Companions, then those who followed them, then those after them. The Prophet (peace be upon him) testified, "The best of people are my generation, then those who come after them, then those who come after them" (Sahih al-Bukhari 2652). This protects the religion from being bent to fit the fashions of any later era.

The Five Pillars of Islam

The outward structure of the religion is summarised in a famous hadith where the Prophet (peace be upon him) said, "Islam is built upon five: to testify that there is no god but Allah and that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah, to establish the prayer, to give the zakah, to fast Ramadan, and to perform Hajj to the House for whoever is able" (Sahih al-Bukhari 8).

  • The Shahada. Bearing witness that none has the right to be worshipped but Allah, and that Muhammad is His Messenger. This is the doorway into Islam.
  • The Salah. Five daily prayers that anchor a Muslim's day and keep the heart connected to Allah.
  • The Zakah. An obligatory portion of accumulated wealth given to the poor and other eligible categories, purifying both the wealth and the giver.
  • The Fast of Ramadan. Abstaining from food, drink, and intimacy from dawn to sunset for a month, training the soul in patience and God-consciousness.
  • The Hajj. The pilgrimage to Makkah, obligatory once in a lifetime upon those who have the physical and financial means.

These pillars are not rituals for their own sake. Each one trains the believer in obedience, gratitude, and sincerity.

The Six Articles of Faith

If the five pillars are the body of Islam, the six articles of faith (iman) are its creed. When the angel Jibril asked the Prophet (peace be upon him) about faith, he answered, "It is to believe in Allah, His angels, His books, His messengers, the Last Day, and to believe in the decree, both its good and its bad" (Sahih Muslim 8).

So a Muslim believes in Allah and His perfect oneness, in the angels He created from light, in the revealed books including the Torah, the Injil, and the final Qur'an that supersedes them, in all the prophets and messengers, in the Day of Resurrection and the reckoning that follows, and in the divine decree, that nothing happens except by Allah's knowledge and will. Faith and action go together. Belief in the heart is confirmed by the tongue and shown in deeds.

How a Person Enters Islam

Becoming a Muslim is simple and immediate. There is no ceremony, no clergy, and no fee. A person declares with sincerity and understanding the two testimonies: that none deserves worship but Allah, and that Muhammad is His Messenger.

What matters is sincerity and intention. The Prophet (peace be upon him) said, "Actions are but by intentions, and every person will have only what he intended" (Sahih al-Bukhari 1). When a person accepts Islam sincerely, Allah forgives what came before it. From there, the new Muslim learns to pray, purifies his worship for Allah alone, and builds his knowledge step by step.

Islam, Marriage, and Family

Islam does not treat worship as something confined to the mosque. It shapes how a person eats, earns, speaks, and marries. Marriage in particular is given weight as half of a believer's practice of the religion and a path to tranquillity and chastity.

Allah describes the purpose of marriage: "And of His signs is that He created for you from yourselves mates that you may find tranquillity in them, and He placed between you affection and mercy" (Qur'an 30:21). The Prophet (peace be upon him) advised choosing a spouse for faith above all else: "A woman is married for four things: her wealth, her lineage, her beauty, and her religion. So choose the one of religion, may your hands be rubbed with dust" (Sahih al-Bukhari 5090).

Islam gives this process a framework rather than leaving it to free-mixing and dating. Common Misconceptions About Islam

That Islam is a new or regional religion. Islam teaches that the worship of Allah alone is the original religion of all the prophets. Muhammad (peace be upon him) did not found a new faith; he completed and restored the message.

That any inherited custom counts as the religion. Much of what people assume is Islam is actually culture. The religion is only what is established in the Qur'an and the authentic Sunnah as understood by the Salaf. Customs that contradict the texts are not part of the deen.

That Islam can be reshaped to fit the times. Allah perfected the religion already. Attempts to reinterpret clear texts to suit secular or progressive fashions are not reform; they are a departure from what was revealed. The religion judges the age, not the reverse.

That faith is private sentiment with no demands. Islam is submission. It asks for belief and obedience, prayer and zakah, honesty and modesty. It is a complete way of life, not a label.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does the word Islam mean? Islam means submission and surrender to Allah with obedience and sincerity. A Muslim is one who submits to Allah alone and follows His Messenger Muhammad (peace be upon him).

What are the core beliefs of Islam? The foundation is tawhid, worshipping Allah alone without partners. Beyond that, a Muslim affirms the six articles of faith: belief in Allah, His angels, His books, His messengers, the Last Day, and the divine decree.

What is the difference between the five pillars and the six articles of faith? The five pillars are the main acts of worship: the testimony of faith, prayer, zakah, fasting Ramadan, and Hajj. The six articles of faith are the creed a Muslim believes in the heart. Islam requires both belief and action.

How does someone become a Muslim? By sincerely declaring the two testimonies of faith: that none has the right to be worshipped but Allah, and that Muhammad is His Messenger. There is no ritual or intermediary required, only sincere belief followed by acting on it.

Is Islam the same message earlier prophets brought? Yes in its core. Every prophet called to the worship of Allah alone. Islam teaches that Muhammad (peace be upon him) is the final messenger and the Qur'an is the final, preserved revelation for all people until the Last Day.

How do I know what Islam really teaches versus culture? Measure everything against the Qur'an and authentic Sunnah as understood by the first three generations. If a practice has no basis in those sources, it is custom, not religion. For anything unclear or contested, ask a qualified scholar rather than relying on assumption.
