Online Nikah: How It Works and Whether It's Valid

﷽

In the name of Allah, the Most Merciful, the Bestower of Mercy.

We try to follow and explain from the Qur'an and the authentic Hadith, upon the understanding of the three rightly-guided generations (the Salaf as-Salih). All good is from Allah alone, and any mistake or shortcoming is from ourselves.

TL;DR: An online nikah can be valid when the same conditions that govern any marriage are met in full: the woman's walī, two upright Muslim witnesses, a clear offer and acceptance (ījāb and qabūl), agreed mahr, and the free consent of both parties. The distance is not the problem. The problem is only ever a missing pillar or an unverified identity. Where those are secured, the marriage stands; where they are loose, it is doubtful.

What an "online nikah" actually is

An online nikah is not a different kind of marriage. It is an ordinary Islamic marriage contract in which some of the people involved are connected by live video or telephone rather than sitting in one room. The bride may be in one country, the groom in another, and the walī and witnesses spread further still. Technology carries the voices. It does not change a single rule.

So the honest question is never "is online marriage a special category?" It is: "were the pillars and conditions of nikah fulfilled?" If yes, the fact that a screen was involved changes nothing. If a pillar was skipped because people assumed the internet made it easier, the marriage is defective for that reason, not because of the internet.

This is why serious couples treat an online nikah with the same weight as any other. The contract you are entering is the same solemn covenant Allah called a mīthāqan ghalīẓā, a solemn pledge (Qur'an 4:21).

The pillars that must be present, screen or no screen

A nikah is not valid on good intentions alone. It has fixed pillars, and every one of them must be secured before anyone celebrates.

1. The walī

A woman does not marry herself off. Her nikah is contracted by her walī, ordinarily her father, then the nearest male relatives in order. The Prophet ﷺ said there is no marriage without a walī, reported in Sunan Abu Dawud 2085 and graded authentic. He also said, in a narration in Sunan Ibn Majah 1879, that any woman who marries without the permission of her walī, her marriage is invalid.

This condition is not negotiable and no online arrangement removes it. If a platform, an imam, or a website tells a sister she can dispense with her guardian because "it's just online," walk away. We cover the guardian's role in detail in our guide to the wali in marriage.

2. Two witnesses

The contract must be witnessed by two trustworthy Muslim men. A marriage conducted in secret, with no witnesses, is not the nikah the Sharī'ah recognises. In an online setting the witnesses must actually hear the offer and acceptance as they happen, whether they are physically beside a party or joined to the same live call.

3. Offer and acceptance (ījāb and qabūl)

The walī offers the marriage and the groom accepts, clearly and unconditionally, in words everyone present understands. This is the actual moment the contract is concluded. It must be unambiguous and it must be heard by the witnesses.

4. Mahr

The mahr is the bridal gift due to the wife. Allah commands, "And give the women their bridal gifts graciously" (Qur'an 4:4). It should be named and agreed. It need not be large; the Prophet ﷺ approved a marriage where the man had little to give, as in Sahih al-Bukhari 5087.

5. Consent

Both parties must genuinely consent. A woman cannot be forced. The Prophet ﷺ said a previously married woman must not be married until she is consulted, and a virgin until her permission is sought, reported in Sahih al-Bukhari 5136. Consent given under pressure, whether in a hall or over a video link, is no consent.

Is an online nikah valid? The considered answer

Here is where you need honesty rather than a slogan. The classical scholars laid down that the ījāb and qabūl should take place in one sitting, the majlis al-'aqd, so that the offer and acceptance are clearly connected and heard together. The dispute among contemporary scholars is whether a live video or phone call counts as "one sitting" when the people are physically apart.

The stronger and widely held position among reliable contemporary scholars is this: a live, real-time connection where all parties hear one another, the offer and acceptance follow immediately, the witnesses hear it directly, and every identity is verified beyond doubt, fulfils the purpose of the single sitting. On this view the online nikah is valid. Fiqh councils that have looked at modern communications have leaned this way for contracts in general, provided identities are certain.

The caution that some scholars raise is not really about the internet. It is about two practical dangers:

  • Identity fraud. On a phone or a screen it is easier to impersonate a walī or fake a witness. The Sharī'ah cannot be built on "probably him."
  • A broken session. If the offer is made, the call drops, and acceptance comes an hour later in a different mood and setting, the connection between offer and acceptance is weakened.

Because reasonable scholars differ on the edges of this, do not treat it as trivially settled. Secure the marriage the safest way you can, and if your situation is complicated, put it to a qualified scholar rather than to a forum. For the wider picture of the contract itself, see our guide to the nikah contract.

How to conduct an online nikah properly

If you are going to do this, do it so that no honest scholar could fault it.

  1. Confirm the walī. Establish who the rightful walī is and that he genuinely agrees to contract the marriage. If he is absent or unjustly refusing, that is a separate matter to be resolved correctly, not bypassed.
  2. Verify every identity. Everyone on the call should be known and confirmed: the groom, the walī, the two witnesses, and the bride. Use a live video connection, not a recording, and not a chain of messages.
  3. Gather the witnesses on the same live link. Two trustworthy Muslim men must actually hear the offer and acceptance in real time.
  4. Agree and state the mahr before or during the session, clearly.
  5. Perform the ījāb and qabūl in one connected session. The walī offers, the groom accepts immediately and clearly, all in a single unbroken sitting with the witnesses listening.
  6. Document it. Record the names, the date, the witnesses, and the mahr in writing, and register the marriage with the relevant civil authority where you live. Documentation is not a pillar, but it protects the wife's rights and shields the couple from later disputes and accusations.

A khutbah before the contract (Khutbat al-Ḥājah) is a recommended Sunnah, but its absence does not invalidate the marriage.

What an online nikah does not fix

Be clear about its limits. An online contract makes two people lawfully married, but it does not by itself make them a household living together. Where the couple is separated by borders, they still need to plan the walīmah and the actual coming together, and they must not fall into private, unsupervised contact before the contract is genuinely concluded. The permission to be alone and intimate follows a valid nikah, not the intention to have one.

It also does not launder a bad match. A valid contract over a beautiful video call is still a poor decision if the man's dīn and character were never checked. The Prophet ﷺ instructed that when someone whose religion and character please you proposes, you should accept, reported in Sunan al-Tirmidhi 1084. A word on marriage as worship

Allah made this bond a source of tranquillity: "And among His signs is that He created for you mates from among yourselves, that you may find rest in them, and He placed between you affection and mercy" (Qur'an 30:21). The Prophet ﷺ described marriage as completing half of one's religion, in a narration in [Sunan Ibn Majah 1846] and elsewhere; the well-known wording is that a servant who marries has safeguarded half of his dīn.

So whether your contract is signed across a table or across an ocean, treat it as an act of worship. Fulfil the conditions Allah set, keep the rights of the other party, and enter it seeking His pleasure. That intention, more than any convenience of technology, is what carries a marriage.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is an online nikah valid in Islam?

It can be, when the pillars are fully met: the walī, two Muslim witnesses, a clear offer and acceptance in one connected session, agreed mahr, and mutual consent, with every identity verified. The stronger contemporary view accepts a live real-time contract on these conditions. If any pillar is missing or an identity is unverified, its validity becomes doubtful. Ask a qualified scholar about your specific case.

Can the walī give permission over the phone or video?

Yes, provided his identity is certain and he genuinely and clearly consents in the live session. What is not acceptable is proceeding without a rightful walī at all, or relying on a vague message that cannot be confirmed to be from him.

Do we still need two witnesses if the nikah is online?

Absolutely. Two trustworthy Muslim men must hear the offer and acceptance as they happen, on the same live connection. A witnessless secret marriage is not the nikah the Sharī'ah recognises.

Is a recorded or text-message nikah valid?

This is far weaker and best avoided. The contract should be a live, connected exchange so the offer and acceptance are clearly linked and heard by the witnesses in one session. Recordings and message chains introduce delay and doubt. Use a live call.

Does an online nikah need to be registered with the government?

Registration is not a pillar of the Islamic contract, but you should still do it. Legal documentation protects the wife's mahr and rights, secures inheritance and residency matters, and prevents future disputes. Fulfil the Sharī'ah conditions and the civil requirements both.

Can we talk privately or live together right after an online nikah?

Only once the nikah is genuinely valid and concluded does the couple become lawful to one another. Before that, the ordinary limits on private, unsupervised contact still apply. And a valid contract does not remove the practical need to plan the walīmah and the coming together responsibly.
